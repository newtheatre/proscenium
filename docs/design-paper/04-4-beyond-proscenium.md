# Beyond Proscenium

Ten capabilities that Proscenium lacks decide whether a second theatre can adopt the platform. Each is listed with why it matters and what it asks of the core.

| Capability | Why a general platform needs it | What it asks of the core |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Production management (lowest priority) | Proposal, casting, rehearsals and budget belong with the show's tickets and rota | Per-record permissions; a production team as a source of authority |
| Online payment | Most theatres sell online; NNT's reader-only rule is unusual | A provider interface; refunds by original method |
| Allocated seating | Any venue with numbered seats | A seating plan per space; seat-level capacity constraints |
| Inventory | Props, costume and kit are a theatre's main physical assets | Bookable resources that are not rooms |
| Fundraising | Charities and community theatres depend on it | Donor consent and tax-relief records as protected data |
| Visiting companies and hires | Receiving houses and societies that host others | An external party scoped to its own production |
| Localisation | Adoption outside the UK | Translatable strings, currency and time zone as settings |
| Access bookings | A legal and ethical baseline | Encrypted special-category data; companion entitlements |
| Calendar feeds and a public API | People live in their own calendars; other sites list the shows | Stable public identifiers; token-scoped feeds |
| Several venues | Companies that perform in more than one place | Venue as its own record on every performance |

## Smaller gaps worth closing

- A member's own history: past bookings, shifts worked, shows credited.
- Wallet tickets, where a theatre can supply a signing certificate.
- A read-state and unread count in the inbox.
- Editing of content pages by people who do not use Git.
- Demo data for evaluation and training.
- Import from spreadsheets for people, productions and stock, so no theatre needs a bespoke migration.
