# 12. As built

Where the build departs from, or is narrower than, parts 1 to 11. The build is the reference from here on.

## The plan: BC-03, BC-04, BC-06 to BC-10, BC-12, BC-13 (24 November 2026)

- **Module.** `layers/budgets`, requiring `finance`. It declares only what is built: abilities `budgets.budget.view`, `budget.manage`, `budget.approve` and `category.manage`. `budget.hold`, the claim and request abilities and the settings come with the slices that use them, as the conformance check requires. The treasurer's role template gains the four.
- **Accounts (BC-04).** `budgets.claims-payable` (liability) and `budgets.expenses` (expense), made by Finance's own listener when the module is switched on.
- **Categories (BC-03).** Seeded by the listener `categories` on `core.module.enabled`: Set, Costume, Lighting, Sound, Publicity, Rights and licences, Travel, Hospitality, Equipment, General. A category's account is an active expense account given to Budgets (`module = 'budgets'`), or none, which posts to `budgets.expenses`. Admin > Modules > Spending categories edits and archives them.
- **Departments.** Core's shared vocabulary `core.department` is one list in two headings (R3-07, Matt's answer to Q3 on 24 November): Production (creative, cast, stage management, lighting, sound, video, set, props, costume, hair and make-up, music, production) and Organisation (technical, publicity, front of house, bar, social, general). A term's kind is its heading's fixed key, `production` or `organisation`: core's term headings gain a `key` column, set from the declaration's `groupKeys`, and a keyed heading can be renamed but not removed. Core's `listTerms(vocabularyKey, { groupKey })` reads the terms of one kind; Budgets offers `listTerms('core.department', { groupKey: 'organisation' })`.
- **The finance year.** Finance gains `financeYearOf(date)`, the year from `finance.year.starts` that holds a London date. A production's budget takes the year of its first performance (today when it has none), a season's the year it starts, and a department or general budget the year chosen on the form.
- **`budgets_budgets` adds two columns** to part 2: `proposed_by`, so the proposer cannot approve (checked on the statement through the account scope, so a merged account is the same person), and `note`, the latest note from sending back or reopening, shown at the top of the budget. The audit keeps that each happened.
- **Owner rule.** The kit's `owner: 'refuses'` needs a record with an owner column, which a budget does not have. The four-eyes rule on approval is the service's own check, so the Owner is refused like anyone else (PR 643).
- **Lines.** Changed only while the budget is a draft, with `budgets.budget.manage`. On an approved budget the approver may add a line (approved at once, with a reason), rename it, change its category or sources, revise its amount (BC-09) or archive it; a line with spending, commitments or pending money on it is not archived. A proposed or closed budget's lines do not change.
- **Figures (BC-10).** Read, never stored. Spent is Finance's new `budgetLineTotals(lineIds)`: sealed entries tagged with the line, debits positive. Committed is, for now, only budgets funded from the line (BC-12): their approved expense total once approved or closed. Pending is 0 until claims and bills exist. An income line's actual is what its source accounts took for the budget's production (by `production_id`), or, for other budgets, over the season's dates or the finance year, plus any income entry tagged with the line.
- **Revising below spent plus committed** is refused `budgets.line.below-spent` with the floor in `detail.floorMinor`; sending `confirm` sets it anyway.
- **Closing (BC-13).** Close has nothing to wait for yet; requests, claims and awards will each add their guard. The `budgets.close.after` offer comes with the production tab. Reopening is refused `budgets.budget.taken` when another open budget has taken the same production, season or department and year.
- **Not yet built from this release:** claims (BC-19 on), BC-18 and the awards.

## Holders, the production's Budget tab and the offer to close: BC-11, BC-14, BC-13 (24 November 2026)

- **Holders (BC-14).** A fact, not a role (decision 0009, R3-11): `budgets.holder-of` on `budgets.budget`, read at each request from the new table `budgets_holders` (budget, account, who added them and when), and mapped by default to `budgets.budget.hold`, which implies `budget.view` on that budget. Whoever holds `budgets.budget.manage` adds and removes holders on the budget page, not themselves (`budgets.holder.self`); removing one takes effect on their next request. A closed budget's holders are shown but not changed. The person picker searches through Budgets' own `people` route under `budget.manage`. The budget says "You hold this budget" to a holder. The treasurer's template carries `budget.hold` site-wide.
- **Erasure.** Budgets' `core.erasure-handler` deletes an erased person's `budgets_holders` rows, so an anonymised account never holds a budget. The table has no id column.
- **Reaching a held budget.** A holder sees the budget they hold, not the Budgets list, which stays with site-wide `budget.view`. Approvals (from BC-15) is where holders start.
- **The production lead.** Part 6's suggestion that a production lead holds their production's budget through `core.production-team` is not wired: that fact is about productions, not budgets. The lead is named a holder of the budget meanwhile.
- **The production's Budget tab and fact (BC-11).** On `core.production`, for `budgets.budget.view`. They show the production's open budget (or its newest closed one) with its totals and lines, income lines with ticket and bar income to date, and a link to it. With no budget, a manager is offered to make one, which opens the new budget form on that production.
- **The offer to close (BC-13).** Setting `budgets.close.after` (60 days, 7 days to a year, group `close`, changed with the new `budgets.settings.manage`). An approved production budget whose production's last performance ended longer ago than that says so on the budget page and the tab. Closing stays a person's choice.

## Spending requests and Approvals: BC-15 to BC-17 (24 November 2026)

- **Table.** `budgets_spending_requests` (prefix `bgr`) as part 2, with `updated_at` as the last event that retention counts from. The requester is aliased on a merge (reads go through the account scope), and a request with none is listed to staff.
- **Abilities.** `budgets.request.make` (site), and `budgets.claim.approve-any` (privileged), declared now because it is what approves over budget (C3). What it implies (`claim.view-all`) comes with claims. The four-eyes rule is the service's, through the account scope, on the statement as well as before it.
- **Asking (BC-15).** Members' area, Expenses, "Ask to spend": a live expense line of an approved budget, an amount, what for (up to 500 characters) and an optional needed-by date. The budget's holders are told by `budgets.request-to-approve`, a message type the spec's table lacks; it mirrors `budgets.claim-to-approve` and goes to whoever holds `budget.hold` on the budget, the requester left out.
- **Deciding (BC-16).** Console, Approvals: requests on budgets the person holds, or every request for a holder of `claim.approve-any`, never their own, oldest first. The menu entry has no ability, because holders hold budgets one at a time; it shows to whoever has something waiting (`count` with `hideEmpty`). Approving over budget is refused `budgets.request.over-budget` with `detail.overMinor` unless the approver holds `claim.approve-any`; declining needs a note. Approval sets `lapses_on` from `budgets.requests.lapse-after` (60 days) and the requester is told by `budgets.request-decided`. An approved request's amount less what has fulfilled it counts as committed on its line.
- **Withdrawing.** By the requester, a holder, or the treasurer, while nothing has fulfilled it.
- **The daily task (BC-17).** `budgets.sweep-requests` at 04:50 lapses approved requests past `lapses_on` and, after `budgets.retention.requests` (2 years) from a request's last event, clears its requester, description and note.
- **Closing.** Refused `budgets.budget.open-requests` while a request on the budget is waiting or approved.
- **Not built: BC-18.** The licence-fee listener waits for the Productions module, which does not exist yet; the `source_*` columns and their unique index are in place for it. `fulfilRequest` comes with claims and bills.

## Claims: BC-19 to BC-25, BC-27 and BC-28 (24 November 2026)

- **Tables.** `budgets_claims` (`ecl`), `budgets_claim_items` (`eci`), `budgets_claim_events` (`ece`, append-only) and `budgets_counters`, as part 2, with three differences. `number` is null while a draft and set at the first submission (a check requires it on every state but draft and withdrawn). `claimant_id` is nullable, so a claim can be anonymised, and is listed to staff when unlinked. Items carry `sort_order` and timestamps. The claimant's words (title, item descriptions, reasons, event notes) are `retain` until `budgets.retention.claims`, reason `budgets.retention.financial-record`.
- **Abilities.** `budgets.claim.make` (site) and `budgets.claim.view-all` (privileged, `audit: 'read'`, no fresh sign-in, since it only reads); `claim.approve-any` now implies `view-all`. A read of a claim by someone who neither made it nor holds one of its budgets is audited as `read` under `view-all`. The treasurer's template gains `claim.approve-any`. Members are given `claim.make` and `request.make` through a role for now: core has no way for a module to map an ability to the `core.member` fact.
- **Settings.** `claims.receipt-required` (on), `claims.late-after` (90 days), `requests.expected-above` (unset) and `retention.claims` (6 years, confirm on change), all quotable apart from retention. `claims.routes`, `claims.default-route` and `claims.second-approval-above` come with paying (BC-26, BC-29 on); until then every claim's route is `bank-transfer`.
- **Making a claim (BC-19).** Members' area, Expenses, "Make a claim": a title and, optionally, one of the person's approved requests; the draft opens on its own page. Items have a date (not in the future), what was bought, where from, the amount, a live expense line of an approved budget and a category (the line's unless another is chosen). Receipts are uploaded through Budgets' own route into core's slot `budgets.receipt` (owner `budgets.claim-item`, images and PDF, at most five an item, the document size limit), so only the claimant of a draft or returned claim can add one; core checks each file. The camera opens first on a phone (`capture="environment"`). `core.file-access` lets the claimant, an approver of the claim's lines and `view-all` read them.
- **Submitting (BC-20).** Needs at least one item, a line of an approved budget on every item, a receipt or a reason on every item while `receipt-required` is on, and the declaration ticked. The first submission takes the finance year's next number ("2026-0042") by a conditional raise of `budgets_counters` in the same batch. Holders of the claim's budgets are told by `budgets.claim-to-approve`, or the holders of `claim.approve-any` when the budgets have none. Items and the title change only while draft or returned (`budgets.claim.fixed`).
- **Reviewing and approving (BC-21, BC-22).** Approvals lists claims beside requests, with the over-budget amount and the marks. The review page shows each item with its receipts; the approver confirms each item's line and category and the amount, and less than the receipt needs a reason. An approver decides every waiting item on the budgets they hold in one go (`budgets.claim.undecided` otherwise). On a claim across two budgets the first holder's approval is logged with no change of state; the approval that leaves nothing undecided posts the expense and approves the claim. C1 refuses the claimant and the requester of the request it fulfils (`budgets.claim.own`), the Owner included; C3 refuses a holder `budgets.claim.over-budget` with `detail.overMinor`; C5 predicates every write on `state = 'submitted'` and the claim's `version`, which every action raises (`budgets.claim.changed`). Approving nothing at all is refused: decline instead.
- **What approval posts.** One transfer, purpose `expense`, key `budgets:claim:<id>:expense`, dated at approval: a debit per item to its category's account (or `budgets.expenses`), tagged with its line and the budget's production, and a credit to `budgets.claims-payable` for the total. The line's Pending figure is now what submitted claims ask of it, and Spent includes approved claims through the ledger.
- **Sending back and declining (BC-23).** Both need a note the claimant sees and clear any approvals made so far; resubmission is logged as `resubmitted`. Declining posts nothing.
- **Withdrawing (BC-24)** by the claimant while draft, submitted or returned. **Voiding (BC-25)** by a holder of `claim.approve-any`, not on their own claim, with a note, while nothing is paid: Finance's `reversePosting` in the same unit.
- **Requests (BC-27).** A claim may name one of the claimant's approved requests; approval raises its fulfilled amount up to the request's amount and marks it fulfilled when reached; a void lowers it again.
- **Marks (BC-28).** Items spent before `late-after` ahead of the submission are marked late; a claim above `requests.expected-above` with no request is marked "not asked first". Neither refuses.
- **Closing a budget** is refused `budgets.budget.open-claims` while a claim on one of its lines is submitted or returned.
- **Erasure.** `core.erasure-hold` holds `budgets.open-claim` (submitted or returned) and `budgets.unpaid-claim` (approved and not fully paid). Erasure clears the claimant, words and receipts of drafts, withdrawn and declined claims at once; approved, paid and voided claims wait for retention (BC-33, with paying).
- **Not yet built** in this part: see the next section.

## Second approval, routes and paying: BC-26, BC-29 to BC-32 (24 November 2026)

- **Second approval (BC-26, C4).** `claims.second-approval-above` (money, unset, £1 to £100,000, quotable). When the approval that leaves nothing undecided brings the claim's approved total above it, nothing is posted: `budgets_claims.first_approved_by` (new, nullable, an actor reference) records who approved, the claim stays submitted, and the holders of `claim.approve-any` other than that person and the claimant are told by `budgets.claim-to-approve`. The second approval is an approve with no items, by a holder of `claim.approve-any` who is not the first approver (`budgets.claim.second` otherwise); it posts the expense as any approval does. Sending back or declining clears `first_approved_by`. Approvals lists a claim waiting on a second approval to those who may give it; the review page says it is waiting, and to whom.
- **Routes.** `claims.routes` (a list of bank-transfer, parent-body, cash and cheque; bank transfer by default) and `claims.default-route` (bank transfer), both staff. Two setting rules: at least one route is offered, and the usual route is one of them. A new claim takes the usual route; the claimant changes it on the claim while it is a draft or sent back, among those offered (`budgets.claim.route`). A claim paid by transfer points to the person's bank details page (Payments, P-A1).
- **Paying (BC-29 to BC-31).** Budgets has `uses: ['payments']`, not `requires`: it contributes the `payments.disbursement` handler for `budgets.claim` and never imports Payments. Its screen, Money > Payments to make (`budgets.claim.pay`, new, privileged, implies `view-all`), lists approved claims still owed, never the viewer's own, grouped by claimant and route, with "Bank details on file, ending 78" or "No bank details yet" from Payments. Choosing claims of one group and "Record payment" posts to Payments' `POST /api/payments/disbursements`, one part per claim for what is still owed, by the method that matches the route (cash is petty cash), dated and with a reference. The handler refuses anyone without `claim.pay` (`budgets.claim.not-payer`), the claimant (`budgets.claim.own`) and a part above what is owed (`budgets.claim.overpaid`, a predicate on the update); it raises `paid_minor`, logs `paid` with the disbursement, debits `budgets.claims-payable`, and when the claim is all paid marks it paid and tells the claimant. A reversed payment lowers `paid_minor`, returns a paid claim to approved and logs `payment-reversed`. Part payments are possible through the route; the screen pays what is owed in full.
- **The parent body's schedule (BC-32).** "Download for the parent body", offered while parent-body is a route: CSV of the approved, unpaid parent-body claims, one row per approved item (claim number, claimant, date approved, category, the category account's nominal code, amount, receipt count), formula-injection guarded by Finance's `toCsv`, the download audited as a read under `claim.pay`. The claims are marked paid when the treasurer records the parent body's payment with the method "paid by the parent body".
- **Not yet built:** retention (BC-33), the over-budget notice (BC-34), Reports' measures (BC-35), the period-close check (BC-36) and the export (BC-37).
