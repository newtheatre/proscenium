## Build order

1. **Engine.** The definition format; conditions, routing, calculations and checks; the same cases in every runtime. FB-101 to FB-106.
2. **Builder.** One page; drag and drop; every question type; the options editor; logic; problems; preview; publish; JSON import and export. FB-201 to FB-207, the file half of FB-510.
3. **Answering and responses.** A link with an audience, dates and a cap; fill in and send; drafts; My forms; the responses table and export; a data class and encryption on every answer. FB-301 to FB-304, FB-401, FB-402, FB-601.
4. **Stages, for forms that want them.** FB-403, FB-404.
5. **Review, votes and decisions.** FB-405 to FB-407, with change 1.
6. **Plugging into other modules.** Slots, the slot renderer, sources from other modules, outcome actions. FB-501, FB-502, FB-504, FB-506.
7. **Finishing.** Templates, the retention sweep, record mode, undo and redo, version history. FB-208, FB-503, FB-602.

## The engine as built (slice 1)

The engine is `layers/forms/shared/engine`, imported by the builder, the page a respondent fills in
and the server alike. It imports nothing from the platform.

- `parseDefinition(input, limits)` reads a definition or says where it departs from the format.
- `evaluate(definition, answers, context)` returns what is visible, required and wrong, the
  calculated values, the route through the pages and the answers with every hidden one removed.
  The server stores that last value and nothing the browser worked out.
- `checkDefinition(definition, options)` is the problems list. The two "can never be reached"
  warnings of part 3 are not in it yet; they arrive with the builder.
- `CASES` is the shared case set. It runs in Node and in the Worker runtime on every change; the
  run in a browser is added with the builder, which is the first page to load the engine.
- A choice's answer is its option's id; an "Other" answer is the id `other` with its text held
  beside it. Money is whole minor units. Dates are calendar dates with no time zone; the platform
  passes in the operating day as `today`, and the engine never reads a clock.

## Forms, drafts and versions as built (slice 2, storage)

The service is `layers/forms/server/forms.ts`; the routes are under `/api/forms/forms`.

- **A form has one draft and any number of published versions.** The draft is its own row
  (`forms_drafts`), so `forms_versions` holds published versions only and is append-only: the
  database refuses any change to one. Part 2 kept the draft as a version with a status; the kit can
  make a whole table immutable but not some of its rows, so the draft moved out. "Superseded" is not
  stored: a version is superseded when it is no longer the form's live one.
- **A question is identified by its form and its id in the definition** (`forms_fields`), not by a
  site-wide id. A form exported as JSON can then be imported any number of times, here or elsewhere.
- **Saving.** `PUT …/draft` sends the whole definition with the revision it was edited from. A stale
  revision is refused and names who saved last. A save is not an audit entry; a publish is.
- **Publishing.** `GET …/publish` says what publishing would do and writes nothing: the problems,
  anything that stops it, and each change classed safe, caution or breaking. `POST …/publish` makes
  the next version. It refuses a changed type, a lowered data class, an export name another question
  of the form has used, personal answers with no retention chosen (while the setting asks for one),
  a draft with nothing changed, and a breaking change sent without `confirm`.
- **Import and export.** `GET …/export` returns `{ file: 'proscenium.form', fileVersion: 1, title,
  mode, definition }`. Starting a form with a `definition` imports it.
- **Not yet:** the number of answers a retired question holds (there are no answers until slice 3),
  checking a record question's source against the sources that exist (slice 6), and pausing,
  closing and archiving a form.

## The builder as built (slice 2, screen)

The screens are `/console/forms` (the list) and `/console/forms/<id>` (the builder). The editing
functions are `layers/forms/shared/builder/edit.ts`, pure and unit tested; the screen is
`FormsBuilder.vue` and the components beside it, with `useFormBuilder` holding the draft.

- **One page.** Pages and endings down the left, the selected page's questions in the middle, the
  selected item's details on the right. Below 1280 pixels the details open over the questions.
- **Dragging** uses `@dnd-kit/vue`. A grip moves a question within its page, into or out of a
  repeating group, or onto another page in the outline. Dropped on the right-hand half of another
  question, the two sit side by side. Every move is also in the question's menu (move up, move
  down, move to page, side by side, separate lines), so nothing needs a pointer.
- **Options are rows**, each with a lasting id, reordered by dragging. "Other" is a switch.
- **Logic** is a sentence the builder writes from the condition ("Shown when Which award? is Best
  newcomer"), edited as rows that combine by all, any or none, with groups inside groups up to the
  depth setting. A page's routes are rules of the same kind that lead to a page or an ending.
- **Saving.** Each change saves itself after a short pause; the toolbar says Saved, Saving or Not
  saved. A save refused as stale offers a reload. Undo and redo cover the session.
- **Problems.** The toolbar button counts them and lists each with a link to it; the item wears a
  badge. Added to the engine's checks: a page no route leads to (`never-reached`), a question whose
  condition can never be true (`never-shown`, and `required-never-shown` when it is required), a
  condition group with nothing in it (`empty-condition`) and a repeating group with no questions
  (`no-questions`). The first three are warnings; the last two stop publishing. A draft may now be
  saved while it holds an empty condition group, comparison list or repeating group.
- **Page titles** are needed only once a form has more than one page.
- **Preview** runs the engine on the draft, at phone or laptop width. Nothing entered is kept.
- **Publishing** opens a dialog fed by `GET …/publish`: problems that stop it, each change with its
  class, and a tick box when answers in progress would be lost. A refusal for want of a retention
  period offers the form's settings.
- **JSON.** "Form as JSON" in the More menu shows the definition as text to copy, save as a file,
  or replace from a file or pasted text. What does not match the format is listed and not applied;
  what is applied can be undone. A new form may start from an exported file.
- **Tests.** `tests/e2e/forms-builder.spec.ts` builds, drags, pairs, undoes, previews, publishes
  and round-trips JSON, at laptop and phone width, with axe. It also opens a fixture page that runs
  the engine's case set in the browser, so the browser and the server are held to the same answers.
- **Not yet:** an editor for calculations (they survive in JSON), a free pattern and file-type rules
  in the checks editor, answering file and record questions in the preview (slice 3 and slice 6),
  and pausing, closing and archiving a form.

## Answering and responses as built (slice 3, storage and server)

The service is `layers/forms/server/responses.ts`. The screens that use it follow on their own branch.

- **Tables.** `forms_placements` (where a form is offered), `forms_responses`, `forms_response_drafts`
  (the unsent working copy, sealed whole) and `forms_answers` (one row per question per revision,
  append-only). An answer is keyed by its response, revision, question id and entry, the question id
  being the one in the definition, as in the field catalogue.
- **The link.** `GET` and `PUT /api/forms/forms/:id/link`: who may answer (anyone, or people signed
  in), when it opens and closes, a cap, one response each, and active or paused. A form is offered
  nowhere until it has a link, and giving it one needs `forms.form.publish`. Members-only, role and
  invited audiences are in the table's check but not yet offered.
- **Opening a form.** `GET /api/forms/open/:slug` returns the questions when the form can be
  answered, and otherwise only the reason (paused, not yet open, closed, full, sign in, already
  answered) with what would let the person through.
- **A working copy.** `PUT /api/forms/open/:slug/draft` keeps a signed-in person's answers so far,
  sealed. A person has one per form: a second device carries on with the first. Someone signed out
  keeps theirs on the device (a matter for the screen); the emailed link to carry on elsewhere waits
  for Communications.
- **Sending.** `POST /api/forms/open/:slug/responses`. The browser makes the response's id, so the
  same submission sent twice stores one response. The server runs the engine again, drops what
  logic hides, refuses what is not valid with the path of each fault, and writes the count against
  the cap, the response, its answers, the audit entry and the event `forms.response.submitted` in
  one unit. The cap and one response each are held by the statements themselves. A form with no
  workflow puts a response straight into the stage Received, which is closed, so retention counts
  from then.
- **Who it is from.** Signed in: the account. Signed out: an address the person gives, which also
  stands for them where one response each applies (as a keyed hash). Anonymous: neither is kept,
  and an anonymous form cannot hold people to one response each until the participation register
  (FB-603) is built.
- **Classes and sealing (FB-601).** Each answer carries its question's class. A special-category
  answer is stored only sealed, with nothing in a readable column. On erasure of a person the
  registry clears the response's own personal columns, and forms' erasure handler treats each answer
  by its class: personal becomes the fixed marker, free text is removed, sealed answers are emptied,
  public and internal answers stay, and working copies are deleted. The kit's append-only trigger
  now lets a column classed row by row be emptied or set to an erased marker, and nothing else.
- **Reading.** `GET /api/forms/forms/:id/responses` is the table: a column per question the form
  has ever had (retired ones last and marked), a page of fifty rows, filters from the address by
  date and by one answer. Special answers always show there as restricted. `GET …/responses/:id`
  is one response; with `reveal=1` and `forms.response.read-special` it opens the special answers
  and records that it did. `GET /api/forms/mine` and `/api/forms/mine/:id` are a person's own.
- **Export.** `GET …/responses/export` returns CSV under the same filters, needs
  `forms.response.export`, is audited, and leaves special answers out unless asked for by someone
  who may read them. It is built in the request, in pages, up to `forms.limits.export-rows`, rather
  than on the queue with a file that expires: nothing is left lying in storage, and a larger set is
  exported in parts.
- **Abilities** `forms.response.read`, `forms.response.export` and `forms.response.read-special`
  act on the form, as the specification grants them per form.
- **Not yet:** withdrawing a response (nothing is un-final until stages exist, slice 4), files and
  record answers, finding responses by a person's name, calculated values in the table, and the
  reminder and sweep for untouched working copies (slice 7).

## Answering and responses as built (slice 3, screens)

- **The form's own page** is `/forms/<slug>`, open to the public. A form that cannot be answered now says why and what would let the person through (sign in, come back when it opens), and shows none of its questions. Somebody not signed in gives an email address unless the form is anonymous.
- **Keeping answers.** Signed in, answers save to the account as the person goes and come back on any device. Not signed in, they are kept on the device for seven days, never when the form asks a special-category question, and an anonymous form keeps nothing until it is sent. The page says which of these applies.
- **Sending.** Problems are listed at the top of the page as links to the answers, and the first one takes the focus. After sending the person sees their reference. A form that closed or filled while they were answering shows as it now stands.
- **My forms** (`/account/forms`, with a card on My account) lists what a person has started and sent, and shows what they sent, read-only. It appears once they have one.
- **Sharing** is a dialog in the builder (More, Share; it opens by itself after the first publish): the address, who can answer, opening and closing times, the most responses, one response each, and a switch to pause.
- **Responses** (`/console/forms/<id>/responses`, from the builder's More menu) is a table with a column per question the form has ever had, removed ones marked. Filters (sent from, sent up to, the answer to one question) and the page live in the address. A response opens beside the table. Restricted answers stay shut until somebody allowed asks to see them, and each opening is recorded. Export as CSV carries the same filters.
- **The forms list** now also shows forms a person may only read the responses of.
- A one-page form with no page title shows no page heading when answered.

Departure: the date filters are whole days in the theatre's time zone rather than instants. Owed: file uploads and record questions when answering (slice 6), and editing a sent response when it is returned (slice 4).

## Stages as built (slice 4)

- **Stages are optional.** A form with no workflow behaves as before: a response arrives in Received, which is closed. With a workflow, the definition carries `workflow: { stages, transitions }`. Each stage has a name, a kind (waiting, being considered, back with the person, ended approved, ended declined, closed) and optionally what the person who sent it is told. Each move has a label, where it goes from and to, and who may make it: staff or the person who sent the response.
- **Editing them.** The builder's More menu has Stages. "Add review stages" starts from Received, In review, Returned for changes, Approved and Declined with their moves; "Start with one stage" starts bare. Problems with stages (no name, a move to a stage that is gone, a returned stage with no way back, a stage nothing reaches) are in the Problems list and beside the row.
- **Moving a response.** `POST /api/forms/forms/:id/responses/:response/move` takes a move and an optional note, and needs the new ability `forms.response.move` on the form. Nobody can move their own response. The move holds only if the response is still in the stage and revision the mover saw. Each move is a row in `forms_stage_history` (append-only), is audited, and announces `forms.response.moved`.
- **Return for changes.** A response in a returned stage can be changed by the person who sent it, under My forms: `PUT /api/forms/mine/:id` checks the answers again, stores them as a new revision and makes the form's move back. Staff can read any revision, and answers that differ from the revision before are marked Changed.
- **What people see.** The responses table has a Stage column and filter, and CSV export has the column too. A response's panel shows its stage, the moves open to the reader, the revisions and its history. My forms shows the status the stage gives, and the note when a response is returned.
- **Retention** counts from the day a response reaches a final stage, not from the day it was sent.

Departures and what is owed: the person is not yet told by email when their response moves (it waits on the communications templates, slice 7); somebody who answered signed out cannot send changes until the emailed link of R-19 is built; the note to the person lives on the response, and the history holds no reason of its own until decisions come in slice 5; a move is for staff or the submitter only, with reviewers and votes following in slice 5.
