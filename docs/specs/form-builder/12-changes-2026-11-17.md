## Build order

1. **Engine.** The definition format; conditions, routing, calculations and checks; the same cases in every runtime. FB-101 to FB-106.
2. **Builder.** One page; drag and drop; every question type; the options editor; logic; problems; preview; publish; JSON import and export. FB-201 to FB-207, the file half of FB-510.
3. **Answering and responses.** A link with an audience, dates and a cap; fill in and send; drafts; My forms; the responses table and export; a data class and encryption on every answer. FB-301 to FB-304, FB-401, FB-402, FB-601.
4. **Stages, for forms that want them.** FB-403, FB-404.
5. **Review, votes and decisions.** FB-405 to FB-407, with change 1.
6. **Plugging into other modules.** Slots, the slot renderer, sources from other modules, outcome actions. FB-501, FB-502, FB-504, FB-506.
7. **Finishing.** Templates, the retention sweep, record mode, undo and redo, version history. FB-208, FB-503, FB-602.

## The engine as built (slice 1)

The engine is `layers/forms/shared/engine`, imported by the builder, the page a respondent fills in
and the server alike. It imports nothing from the platform.

- `parseDefinition(input, limits)` reads a definition or says where it departs from the format.
- `evaluate(definition, answers, context)` returns what is visible, required and wrong, the
  calculated values, the route through the pages and the answers with every hidden one removed.
  The server stores that last value and nothing the browser worked out.
- `checkDefinition(definition, options)` is the problems list. The two "can never be reached"
  warnings of part 3 are not in it yet; they arrive with the builder.
- `CASES` is the shared case set. It runs in Node and in the Worker runtime on every change; the
  run in a browser is added with the builder, which is the first page to load the engine.
- A choice's answer is its option's id; an "Other" answer is the id `other` with its text held
  beside it. Money is whole minor units. Dates are calendar dates with no time zone; the platform
  passes in the operating day as `today`, and the engine never reads a clock.

## Forms, drafts and versions as built (slice 2, storage)

The service is `layers/forms/server/forms.ts`; the routes are under `/api/forms/forms`.

- **A form has one draft and any number of published versions.** The draft is its own row
  (`forms_drafts`), so `forms_versions` holds published versions only and is append-only: the
  database refuses any change to one. Part 2 kept the draft as a version with a status; the kit can
  make a whole table immutable but not some of its rows, so the draft moved out. "Superseded" is not
  stored: a version is superseded when it is no longer the form's live one.
- **A question is identified by its form and its id in the definition** (`forms_fields`), not by a
  site-wide id. A form exported as JSON can then be imported any number of times, here or elsewhere.
- **Saving.** `PUT …/draft` sends the whole definition with the revision it was edited from. A stale
  revision is refused and names who saved last. A save is not an audit entry; a publish is.
- **Publishing.** `GET …/publish` says what publishing would do and writes nothing: the problems,
  anything that stops it, and each change classed safe, caution or breaking. `POST …/publish` makes
  the next version. It refuses a changed type, a lowered data class, an export name another question
  of the form has used, personal answers with no retention chosen (while the setting asks for one),
  a draft with nothing changed, and a breaking change sent without `confirm`.
- **Import and export.** `GET …/export` returns `{ file: 'proscenium.form', fileVersion: 1, title,
  mode, definition }`. Starting a form with a `definition` imports it.
- **Not yet:** the number of answers a retired question holds (there are no answers until slice 3),
  checking a record question's source against the sources that exist (slice 6), and pausing,
  closing and archiving a form.

## The builder as built (slice 2, screen)

The screens are `/console/forms` (the list) and `/console/forms/<id>` (the builder). The editing
functions are `layers/forms/shared/builder/edit.ts`, pure and unit tested; the screen is
`FormsBuilder.vue` and the components beside it, with `useFormBuilder` holding the draft.

- **One page.** Pages and endings down the left, the selected page's questions in the middle, the
  selected item's details on the right. Below 1280 pixels the details open over the questions.
- **Dragging** uses `@dnd-kit/vue`. A grip moves a question within its page, into or out of a
  repeating group, or onto another page in the outline. Dropped on the right-hand half of another
  question, the two sit side by side. Every move is also in the question's menu (move up, move
  down, move to page, side by side, separate lines), so nothing needs a pointer.
- **Options are rows**, each with a lasting id, reordered by dragging. "Other" is a switch.
- **Logic** is a sentence the builder writes from the condition ("Shown when Which award? is Best
  newcomer"), edited as rows that combine by all, any or none, with groups inside groups up to the
  depth setting. A page's routes are rules of the same kind that lead to a page or an ending.
- **Saving.** Each change saves itself after a short pause; the toolbar says Saved, Saving or Not
  saved. A save refused as stale offers a reload. Undo and redo cover the session.
- **Problems.** The toolbar button counts them and lists each with a link to it; the item wears a
  badge. Added to the engine's checks: a page no route leads to (`never-reached`), a question whose
  condition can never be true (`never-shown`, and `required-never-shown` when it is required), a
  condition group with nothing in it (`empty-condition`) and a repeating group with no questions
  (`no-questions`). The first three are warnings; the last two stop publishing. A draft may now be
  saved while it holds an empty condition group, comparison list or repeating group.
- **Page titles** are needed only once a form has more than one page.
- **Preview** runs the engine on the draft, at phone or laptop width. Nothing entered is kept.
- **Publishing** opens a dialog fed by `GET …/publish`: problems that stop it, each change with its
  class, and a tick box when answers in progress would be lost. A refusal for want of a retention
  period offers the form's settings.
- **JSON.** "Form as JSON" in the More menu shows the definition as text to copy, save as a file,
  or replace from a file or pasted text. What does not match the format is listed and not applied;
  what is applied can be undone. A new form may start from an exported file.
- **Tests.** `tests/e2e/forms-builder.spec.ts` builds, drags, pairs, undoes, previews, publishes
  and round-trips JSON, at laptop and phone width, with axe. It also opens a fixture page that runs
  the engine's case set in the browser, so the browser and the server are held to the same answers.
- **Not yet:** a free pattern and file-type rules
  in the checks editor, answering file and record questions in the preview (slice 3 and slice 6),
  and pausing, closing and archiving a form.

## Answering and responses as built (slice 3, storage and server)

The service is `layers/forms/server/responses.ts`. The screens that use it follow on their own branch.

- **Tables.** `forms_placements` (where a form is offered), `forms_responses`, `forms_response_drafts`
  (the unsent working copy, sealed whole) and `forms_answers` (one row per question per revision,
  append-only). An answer is keyed by its response, revision, question id and entry, the question id
  being the one in the definition, as in the field catalogue.
- **The link.** `GET` and `PUT /api/forms/forms/:id/link`: who may answer (anyone, or people signed
  in), when it opens and closes, a cap, one response each, and active or paused. A form is offered
  nowhere until it has a link, and giving it one needs `forms.form.publish`. Members-only, role and
  invited audiences are in the table's check but not yet offered.
- **Opening a form.** `GET /api/forms/open/:slug` returns the questions when the form can be
  answered, and otherwise only the reason (paused, not yet open, closed, full, sign in, already
  answered) with what would let the person through.
- **A working copy.** `PUT /api/forms/open/:slug/draft` keeps a signed-in person's answers so far,
  sealed. A person has one per form: a second device carries on with the first. Someone signed out
  keeps theirs on the device (a matter for the screen); the emailed link to carry on elsewhere waits
  for Communications.
- **Sending.** `POST /api/forms/open/:slug/responses`. The browser makes the response's id, so the
  same submission sent twice stores one response. The server runs the engine again, drops what
  logic hides, refuses what is not valid with the path of each fault, and writes the count against
  the cap, the response, its answers, the audit entry and the event `forms.response.submitted` in
  one unit. The cap and one response each are held by the statements themselves. A form with no
  workflow puts a response straight into the stage Received, which is closed, so retention counts
  from then.
- **Who it is from.** Signed in: the account. Signed out: an address the person gives, which also
  stands for them where one response each applies (as a keyed hash). Anonymous: neither is kept,
  and an anonymous form cannot hold people to one response each until the participation register
  (FB-603) is built.
- **Classes and sealing (FB-601).** Each answer carries its question's class. A special-category
  answer is stored only sealed, with nothing in a readable column. On erasure of a person the
  registry clears the response's own personal columns, and forms' erasure handler treats each answer
  by its class: personal becomes the fixed marker, free text is removed, sealed answers are emptied,
  public and internal answers stay, and working copies are deleted. The kit's append-only trigger
  now lets a column classed row by row be emptied or set to an erased marker, and nothing else.
- **Reading.** `GET /api/forms/forms/:id/responses` is the table: a column per question the form
  has ever had (retired ones last and marked), a page of fifty rows, filters from the address by
  date and by one answer. Special answers always show there as restricted. `GET …/responses/:id`
  is one response; with `reveal=1` and `forms.response.read-special` it opens the special answers
  and records that it did. `GET /api/forms/mine` and `/api/forms/mine/:id` are a person's own.
- **Export.** `GET …/responses/export` returns CSV under the same filters, needs
  `forms.response.export`, is audited, and leaves special answers out unless asked for by someone
  who may read them. It is built in the request, in pages, up to `forms.limits.export-rows`, rather
  than on the queue with a file that expires: nothing is left lying in storage, and a larger set is
  exported in parts.
- **Abilities** `forms.response.read`, `forms.response.export` and `forms.response.read-special`
  act on the form, as the specification grants them per form.
- **Not yet:** withdrawing a response (nothing is un-final until stages exist, slice 4), files and
  record answers, finding responses by a person's name, and the
  reminder and sweep for untouched working copies (slice 7).

## Answering and responses as built (slice 3, screens)

- **The form's own page** is `/forms/<slug>`, open to the public. A form that cannot be answered now says why and what would let the person through (sign in, come back when it opens), and shows none of its questions. Somebody not signed in gives an email address unless the form is anonymous.
- **Keeping answers.** Signed in, answers save to the account as the person goes and come back on any device. Not signed in, they are kept on the device for seven days, never when the form asks a special-category question, and an anonymous form keeps nothing until it is sent. The page says which of these applies.
- **Sending.** Problems are listed at the top of the page as links to the answers, and the first one takes the focus. After sending the person sees their reference. A form that closed or filled while they were answering shows as it now stands.
- **My forms** (`/account/forms`, with a card on My account) lists what a person has started and sent, and shows what they sent, read-only. It appears once they have one.
- **Sharing** is a dialog in the builder (More, Share; it opens by itself after the first publish): the address, who can answer, opening and closing times, the most responses, one response each, and a switch to pause.
- **Responses** (`/console/forms/<id>/responses`, from the builder's More menu) is a table with a column per question the form has ever had, removed ones marked. Filters (sent from, sent up to, the answer to one question) and the page live in the address. A response opens beside the table. Restricted answers stay shut until somebody allowed asks to see them, and each opening is recorded. Export as CSV carries the same filters.
- **The forms list** now also shows forms a person may only read the responses of.
- A one-page form with no page title shows no page heading when answered.

Departure: the date filters are whole days in the theatre's time zone rather than instants. Owed: file uploads and record questions when answering (slice 6), and editing a sent response when it is returned (slice 4).

## Stages as built (slice 4)

- **Stages are optional.** A form with no workflow behaves as before: a response arrives in Received, which is closed. With a workflow, the definition carries `workflow: { stages, transitions }`. Each stage has a name, a kind (waiting, being considered, back with the person, ended approved, ended declined, closed) and optionally what the person who sent it is told. Each move has a label, where it goes from and to, and who may make it: staff or the person who sent the response.
- **Editing them.** The builder's More menu has Stages. "Add review stages" starts from Received, In review, Returned for changes, Approved and Declined with their moves; "Start with one stage" starts bare. Problems with stages (no name, a move to a stage that is gone, a returned stage with no way back, a stage nothing reaches) are in the Problems list and beside the row.
- **Moving a response.** `POST /api/forms/forms/:id/responses/:response/move` takes a move and an optional note, and needs the new ability `forms.response.move` on the form. Nobody can move their own response. The move holds only if the response is still in the stage and revision the mover saw. Each move is a row in `forms_stage_history` (append-only), is audited, and announces `forms.response.moved`.
- **Return for changes.** A response in a returned stage can be changed by the person who sent it, under My forms: `PUT /api/forms/mine/:id` checks the answers again, stores them as a new revision and makes the form's move back. Staff can read any revision, and answers that differ from the revision before are marked Changed.
- **What people see.** The responses table has a Stage column and filter, and CSV export has the column too. A response's panel shows its stage, the moves open to the reader, the revisions and its history. My forms shows the status the stage gives, and the note when a response is returned.
- **Retention** counts from the day a response reaches a final stage, not from the day it was sent.

Departures and what is owed: the person is not yet told by email when their response moves (it waits on the communications templates, slice 7); somebody who answered signed out cannot send changes until the emailed link of R-19 is built; the note to the person lives on the response, and the history holds no reason of its own until decisions come in slice 5; a move is for staff or the submitter only, with reviewers and votes following in slice 5.

## Review, votes and decisions as built (slice 5)

- **A stage may ask for review.** A stage of the kind "being considered" carries an optional `review`: its mode (marks against criteria, or a vote), its reviewers (the holders of a role, named people, or both), and for a vote the pass mark (more than half, two thirds, or nobody against, of the votes for and against) and the least number who must vote, abstentions included. Set in the Stages dialog, under the stage.
- **Rounds.** Each time a response arrives in such a stage a round opens (`forms_review_rounds`) with one assignment per reviewer (`forms_review_assignments`), taken from who holds the role at that moment. It closes when the response leaves; a response that comes back starts a new round on its answers as they then stand.
- **Reviewing.** A reviewer finds what they are asked for under My forms, "To review", and opens `/account/forms/review/<id>`: the answers (restricted ones stay shut), their marks or vote, and a comment. Routes: `GET /api/forms/reviews`, `GET` and `PUT /api/forms/reviews/:id`, `POST /api/forms/reviews/:id/conflict`. All are the reviewer's own and nobody else's.
- **Private until released.** Until the chair releases a round, a reviewer sees only their own review and everybody else sees only how many are in. `POST …/responses/:response/release` needs the new ability `forms.response.decide`. After release no review changes; the panel and those who decide see a vote's count or the summary of the marks (average, lowest to highest, weighted total). Who said what goes to the panel for marks; for a vote only to those who decide, and to nobody when the stage holds a secret ballot. A stage may instead review in the open from the start.
- **Conflicts of interest.** A reviewer who sent the response is assigned as conflicted without declaring it. Any reviewer may declare one: what they wrote is removed, and from then every route that returns anything about that response refuses them or leaves it out (the response, the table, export, moving, releasing), whatever else they hold.
- **Decisions.** A move staff make to an approved or declined stage is a decision: it needs `forms.response.decide` (which brings moving and reading), dates the response, and may be set to ask for a reason. The reason is kept on the response for those who decide and is never sent to the person; the note to the person stays separate.
- **Never to the submitter.** What the person who sent a response is sent (their own response, the form's page) no longer carries the form's stages at all, so reviewers' identities and the criteria do not reach them.

Departures and what is owed: the chair is whoever holds `forms.response.decide` on the form, not a separate role; the automatic conflict covers the sender only, with co-authors and people named in a "people involved" question following with sources in slice 6; one move applied to several responses at once, and telling reviewers and the person by email, follow in slice 7; the reason for a decision is the latest one only.

## Sources as built (slice 6, part 1)

- **The point `forms.source`.** A module contributes a source for each kind of record a "record from a source" question may choose from: a key (`core.production`), the translation key of its name, who may search it, a search and a way to read labels by id (`layers/forms/server/points/source.ts`). Core contributes to no module's point, so forms contributes the five for core's records itself: people, productions, performances, venues and spaces.
- **Who sees what.** People are searched as core's person picker searches them and only by somebody who holds `core.account.search`; anybody else is told the question is left for the theatre to fill in. Productions and performances show the published, listed ones to anybody and every one to somebody who works on the programme. Venues and spaces in use are public.
- **Answering.** The person types to search and chooses one record, or several where the question allows. `GET /api/forms/sources/:source` serves the search and the labels of what is already chosen, to anybody answering.
- **Storing.** The answer is the chosen ids. The server checks each id against its source when the response is sent, refuses one the source does not know beside the answer, and keeps the labels as they read then, so the table, export and review show names.
- **The builder** offers the sources of the modules switched on now, and a form that names a source nobody offers cannot be published.
- **People involved.** A question of people may be marked "These people are involved": a reviewer named in the answer is assigned as conflicted when the round opens, as the sender already was (FB-406).

Owed: co-authors of a response (there are none yet), and prefill of a record question from the place a form is attached.

## Slots and outcome actions as built (slice 6, parts 2 and 3)

How another module hosts a form, in short: contribute a slot, place `FormsSlotForms` on the record's page, and contribute an outcome action if something should happen when a response is approved. Awards' nominations are the first use: slot `awards.nomination` on an award type, and action `awards.issue-award`.

- **The point `forms.slot`** (data). A module contributes a place a form can be attached to one of its records: `{ id: '<module>.<name>', subject: '<record type>', attach: '<ability>', label: '<translation key>', limit?, source? }`. `attach` is checked on the record itself. `limit` is the most forms one record carries there at once (one unless said). `source` names a `forms.source` for the subject's records, so the answering page and a response can say what they are about; a slot that names one accepts that those names are shown to whoever may answer.
- **Attaching.** `GET /api/forms/slots/:slot/:subject` lists what is attached and the published forms that could be; `PUT …/:form` attaches one or changes its terms (who may answer, opening and closing, cap, one each, taking responses); `DELETE …/:form` takes it away. An attachment is a row in `forms_placements` like the form's own link, so each has its own count and dates. A form taken away and attached again keeps its place and so its responses. The component `FormsSlotForms` (props `slot-id`, `subject`) is the whole of this for a host's page, and its props are part of forms' interface.
- **Answering.** An attached form is answered at `/forms/<slug>?place=<placement id>`: the same page and routes as a link, with the place named. A form may be attached in several places and have a link of its own as well. Each response keeps `subject_type` and `subject_id`.
- **The point `forms.outcome-action`** (code, `layers/forms/server/points/outcome-action.ts`). A module contributes `{ key, label, slot?, inputs, run(unit, call) }`. The designer adds it to a move in the Stages dialog ("Add something to do next") and chooses the question that fills each input; a required input with no question, a question that does not fit and an action nobody offers are problems that block publishing.
- **Running.** A move that carries actions writes one row per action in `forms_action_runs` and queues the job `forms.run-action`, in the move's own unit of work. The job hands the action the mapped answers (value and words), the response with the record it is about, who made the move and when. What the action adds to the unit commits together with the mark that it ran, so its writes happen exactly once however often the job is tried. An action with a `slot` is skipped for a response sent anywhere else. A special-category answer is never handed over.
- **When it fails.** The run stays waiting while its job retries. Once the job has given up, the response's panel shows it as failed with the reason, and somebody who decides on the form can run it again (`POST …/responses/:response/actions/:run/run`).
- **Decisions are announced.** `forms.response.decided` (response, form, approved or declined), internal, beside `forms.response.moved`.

Departures and what is owed: slots are submission mode only (custom fields on a record are FB-511, later); `scope` is one record only; the slot's `features` list (what a form there may not use) is not built, so a host cannot yet forbid stages or pages; the services `slotForms` and `addResponse` for a host that wants to place the questions inside its own page are not built, a host links to the form's address instead; bindable attributes and module templates are later; `forms.action.failed` is not announced, the response's panel and core's dead-job report show a failure instead; the responses table has no filter by the record a response is about.

## Messages and retention as built (slice 7, part 1)

- **What people are told** (`layers/forms/server/notices.ts`, six types on `core.notification-type`). A receipt when a response is sent (`forms.received`). Word of a move staff make when it changes what the person is told about their response (`forms.status`), or that it has come back for changes with where to make them (`forms.returned`). A request to each reviewer who is to review when a round opens, with the address of their review (`forms.review-requested`); a reviewer with a conflict is not asked.
- **Who and how.** Somebody signed in gets an email and an inbox item. Somebody who answered signed out gets an email at the address they gave (`forms.received-guest`, `forms.status-guest`). An anonymous form tells nobody. Each message is written in the unit of the change itself and holds nothing anybody wrote: it names the form, the reference and the status, and links to the rest. A theatre rewords them as it does any other message.
- **Retention** (`forms.sweep-retention`, daily). A response whose `retention_due_at` has passed is anonymised: it loses who sent it, its personal, free-text and special answers, what staff wrote to the person, the reason for a decision and what reviewers wrote. The row stays, so counts and history read true.

Owed: the note staff write is not in the email (the person reads it under My forms, which somebody signed out cannot yet do, R-19); reminders of an unfinished working copy and of a closing date; a reminder to reviewers who have not reviewed.

## Closing, archiving and one move on several responses as built (slice 7, part 2)

- **A form's life.** `POST /api/forms/forms/:id/status` (ability `forms.form.publish`) takes a form between live, paused, closed and archived. A live form can be paused or closed; a paused one resumed or closed; a closed one opened again or archived. A form that was never published can be archived from draft and comes back a draft; a published one comes back closed. Each change is audited, and closing announces `forms.form.closed`.
- **What each state means.** A paused or closed form keeps its address and says so to whoever opens it; it takes no new responses, while staff carry on with those it has and a person may still send back changes they were asked for. An archived form is gone from its address, cannot be edited, and sits below the divider on the forms list.
- **Archiving waits.** A form is not archived while a response it holds is still waiting on somebody (pending, in review or returned): the refusal says how many.
- **In the builder.** The actions are in the More menu, the state shows beside the version, and each change offers Undo.
- **One move on several responses.** `POST /api/forms/forms/:id/responses/move` takes up to fifty responses and one move. Each response is held to the rules of a single move; one that cannot take it (another stage, the mover's own, a conflict) is left alone and named in the reply with the reason, and the rest move together. A refusal about the mover or what they wrote (may not decide, no reason given) refuses the whole request. The responses table gains a checkbox column for somebody who may move, and offers the moves of the stage every chosen row is in. A response on an earlier version whose stages differ is simply left and named.

## Templates as built (slice 7, part 3)

- **Shipped templates** are files (`layers/forms/shared/templates.ts`): Show proposal (pages, a repeating team list, a budget that totals itself, review stages), Access requirements (special-category answers, a question shown on a condition) and Feedback survey (anonymous, a rating). A unit test holds each to the format and to a problems list with no error (FB-208, 1).
- **The theatre's own** are rows in `forms_templates`, saved from a form's draft with "Save as a template" in the builder's More menu (ability `forms.template.manage`, and the form must be one the person may edit). The copy leaves behind the people the form named as reviewers.
- **Starting from one.** New form offers "Start from": a blank form, or any template, with what it is for and how many questions it has. `POST /api/forms/forms` takes `template`; the form is a copy, remembers the key in `template_key` and owes the template nothing afterwards. The feedback survey starts anonymous.
- **Removing one** is beside the choice in New form, with Undo, which puts back what the reply carried. Forms made from it are untouched.
- Routes: `GET` and `POST /api/forms/templates`, `DELETE /api/forms/templates/:id`.

Owed: templates contributed by other modules (the `forms.template` point), which arrive with the slots they suit; renaming a saved template; choosing starting templates as a setup step.

## Version history as built (slice 7, part 4)

- **The page** is `/console/forms/<id>/versions`, reached from the builder's More menu once the form has been published. Each version shows who published it and when, how many responses were sent on it, and how many questions changed from the one before; the live one is marked.
- **Opening a version** shows what changed from the version before, as the publish dialog listed it, and the form as it was published, which can be tried without keeping anything. The open version is in the address.
- **Make this the draft** saves that version's definition as the draft, with Undo. Nothing is published by it: the usual publish, with its statement of impact against the live version, still stands between the draft and anybody answering.
- Routes: `GET /api/forms/forms/:id/versions` and `GET /api/forms/forms/:id/versions/:number`, for somebody who may edit the form.

## Custom fields on a record as built (slice 7, part 5; FB-503)

- **A record-mode slot.** A `forms.slot` may say `mode: 'record'` and name `edit`, the host's own ability on the record. Such a slot takes forms made in record mode only, and a form people send cannot go in it (nor custom fields in a slot for sent forms).
- **Attached for every record at once.** Custom fields are attached to the slot for every record of its type (the subject `*`), by somebody who holds the slot's `attach` ability outright. `FormsSlotForms` with `subject="*"` is the screen: it lists the sets attached, attaches a published set with no terms to choose, takes one away, and starts a new set, which opens in the builder.
- **In the builder** a set of fields is marked "Custom fields" and has no Share and no Stages. It publishes without a retention period, because what it holds lives and goes with its record; it cannot have stages or ask a special-category question (`forms.publish.record-stages`, `forms.publish.record-special`), and it has no link.
- **On the record.** `FormsRecordFields` (props `slot-id`, `subject`) shows each set with the record's answers and saves them in place. `GET /api/forms/records/:slot/:record` and `PUT /api/forms/records/:slot/:record/:form`, for whoever holds the slot's `edit` ability on that record.
- **How it is kept.** One response per record and set, made on the first save, received and closed throughout, with nobody as its sender and the saver as `entered_by`. Each save is a new revision; a save made from an older one is refused (`forms.record.stale`). Answers are read by question, so a new version of the fields keeps what its remaining questions were given. The responses table of the set lists every record's answers.

Departures and what is owed: a slot is one mode or the other and a record-mode slot is always for every record of the type (the specification's `scope` allows one record too); the slot's `features` list is still not built, so the two things custom fields may not have are held at publish; no host module declares a record-mode slot yet (membership's `membership.member-fields` is the first, and custom fields on a production, a person and a venue are FB-511); a host cannot yet read the values for its own lists or exports; when a record is erased its host must say so for the fields to go with it, which wants an event or a service once there is a host.

## Calculations as built (FB-104)

The engine had calculations from slice 1; this gives them a place on the form, an editor and a
place wherever a response is read.

- **What a calculation carries.** Beside its id, export name, type and expression, an optional
  `label` (the theatre's words, as a question's) and `page`. The types are number, money (whole
  minor units in the theatre's currency), `percent` (new: a share of one, so 0.75 reads as 75%),
  date and text. Both new fields are optional, so every earlier definition still reads.
- **Where it is shown.** A calculation with a page is a read-only line after that page's questions,
  with its name and its value as it is worked out; on no other page. One with no page is not shown to
  the person answering at all. Problems: a shown calculation needs a name (`no-label`), its page must
  exist (`unknown-page`), arithmetic on an answer that is not a number is refused (`not-a-number`, now
  for any answer, not only a group's), and one shown before an answer it reads is given is warned of
  (`shown-before-answered`). Removing a page takes the calculation off it.
- **The editor.** Calculations are listed in the outline under the endings, and the open page shows
  each of its own as a card. The details give the name, how it is shown, the page, and the formula as
  rows: each row reads an answer, a total of a number question across a repeating group, how many
  entries a group has, another calculation or a number, and is joined to the row before by add, take
  away, multiply or divide; multiplying and dividing are worked out first. A switch counts a blank
  answer as nothing (stored as "this answer, or 0 when it is empty"). In a money calculation a number
  added or taken away is typed as money. The rows are `shared/builder/formula.ts`, turned into the
  expression tree and back; a formula written in JSON that the rows cannot show (rounding, dates,
  nesting) is kept as it is and says so, with a way to start it again.
- **Kept and shown.** The server works the values out again on sending and keeps only its own (the
  `calculated` column already held them). They are now in the response beside the table, the
  reviewer's page and the person's own copy (with each page's answers, those on no page under
  "Worked out" for staff only, and only the ones the person was shown on their own copy), a column
  each in the responses table, and in the CSV under their export names (money as units, a share as a
  percentage). A value is shown for the latest revision only. One worked out, directly or through
  another calculation, from a special-category answer is as restricted as that answer: "Restricted"
  in the table, left out of the panel until opened and out of an export unless asked for.
- **Show proposal.** Its total is now "Total budget", money, on the budget page, with blanks counted
  as nothing, so "Rough figures are fine" holds.

Departures and what is owed: the table and the export carry the live version's calculations only; a
condition still cannot pick a calculation in the builder (it can in JSON, and the sentence names it);
a percentage or number has no rounding of its own in the rows (round is kept from JSON).
