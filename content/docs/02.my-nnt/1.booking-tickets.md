Anybody can book tickets: the what's on listing, the show page and the booking form are public,
and no account is needed. A signed-in member's booking attaches to their account and can use a
pass or a verified access profile; a guest gives a name and an email address instead. Either
way the seats are held online and paid for at the theatre. Signed in, **My NNT** carries a
"Next show on sale" tile that opens the show page.

::callout{icon="i-lucide-info" color="info"}
**Nothing is paid online, ever.** Booking online holds your seats; you pay at the box office in
person, on the night, on the Students' Union's card reader. The order stub says "Paid online
£0.00, ever" for exactly this reason.
::

## If something goes wrong

- **"Choose at least one ticket"**: every quantity is still 0. The booking button stays
  pressable so the reason is said out loud rather than left to a greyed-out control.
- **"Tell us the name the booking is under"** or **"Tell us where to send your booking
  reference"**: a guest field is empty. **"That does not look like an email address"** means the
  address has no @ or no domain.
- **"Online orders are capped at N tickets; for a larger party, contact the box office
  directly"**: the order, or one line of it, is over {{ PUBLIC_ORDER_SEAT_CAP }}. The button
  stays disabled until it is under.
- **"Sold out"** with **Join the waiting list**: the house is full. The form does not open;
  [Waiting list](/docs/my-nnt/waiting-list) explains what happens next.
- **"Online booking has closed"**: online booking stops when unpaid bookings are released,
  {{ HOLD_RELEASE_MINUTES_BEFORE }} minutes before curtain, or earlier where the performance has
  its own booking window. The banner quotes the time it closed and says "Tickets are on the door,
  subject to availability". The form does not open.
- **"Booking is not open"**: the performance is cancelled, the show is not on sale, or the
  tickets are sold elsewhere, in which case **Book tickets elsewhere** goes to that box office.
  The banner quotes the reason.
- **"This performance no longer has room for that order"**: the seats went while the form was
  open. Try fewer, or join the waiting list.
- **"Only N companion tickets may be held for this performance"**: the access entitlement is
  used up on an earlier booking.

## Finding a show

1. Open **What's on** from the public site. Every show on sale is listed as a poster card, with
   its dates and whether it is still bookable. When shows are on at more than one venue, a row
   of venue buttons filters the list.
2. Choose a show to open its page.

![What's on with the venue filter (1), a show's poster card (2) and its book button (3)](/images/docs/members/whats-on.png)

1. **Filter by venue** narrows the list to one venue. It only appears when more than one venue
   has something on. Where the chosen venue has nothing on, the page says so and offers **Show
   every venue**, which is a different answer from the season itself being empty.
2. **A show's card** carries the poster, the dates and the state of the run.
3. **Book** opens the show page, or the waiting list when the run is full.

## The show page

The show page carries what somebody decides on: dates, running time, ticket prices and age
guidance at the top, then the description, the interval and latecomer policy, the content
warnings and the list of performances. Any of those four facts nobody has settled reads
"To be confirmed", in those words wherever it appears.

![A show page with the dates (1), the ticket prices (2), the book button (3) and the performances (4)](/images/docs/members/show.png)

1. **Dates** span the first and last night still on offer.
2. **Tickets** lists every price on the run, cheapest last, with its condition ("members", for
   instance) beside it.
3. **Book tickets** goes straight to the first night still bookable here.
4. **Performances** lists every night with its own state and its own button: **Book tickets**
   for a night on sale, **Join the waiting list** for a full one, **Book tickets elsewhere**
   where the tickets are sold by another box office, and nothing at all for a cancelled night.

Content warnings come in three states, and the page says which: somebody has been through the
show and found nothing, nobody has been through it yet, or there is a list. "Nobody has been
through this show yet" means nothing has been checked, not that there is nothing to say. Where a
warning carries a description, it is printed beside the warning rather than hidden behind a
hover, so a phone reads it too.

The **Access** panel says how to tell us what somebody needs: a member records it once on
[Access requirements](/docs/my-nnt/access-requirements) and it travels with every booking;
anybody else speaks to the box office. The booking form itself asks for nothing of the kind.

## Holding the seats

What's on goes straight to this form, past the show page, so **Before you book** sits above the
order stub (after the form on a phone, just before the book button). It gives the show's age
guidance and each content warning with its level ("Strobe lighting", "Gun violence: depicted"),
or says the show has no warnings or has not been assessed yet, read from the same rows as the
show page; **What each warning means, on the show page** opens the rest. The booking page and
the confirmation email repeat it under **Before you come**.

1. Pick a performance. When the run has more than one night on sale, the booking page lists them.
   The one being booked is marked **Booking this night** as well as by its border, and every
   night carries its own state ("Tickets available", "4 tickets left", "Sold out", "Booking
   closed") in the words the show page uses. The venue joins that line only where the run moves
   between venues. Picking another night is a link, so the address always names the night.
2. Choose how many of each ticket type. The stub on the right (below the form on a phone) adds
   up what is to pay at the theatre as you go. One online booking holds up to
   {{ PUBLIC_ORDER_SEAT_CAP }} seats, counted across every line; a larger party talks to the box
   office. Where the run also prices a members-only ticket and nobody is signed in, the form says
   so above the lines and offers **Sign in for member prices**.
3. Not signed in, give your name and the email address the booking should go to. Signed in,
   there is nothing to give: the booking attaches to the account.
4. Press **Book your seats**, which names the count once tickets are chosen ("Book 3
   tickets"). The screen confirms with a reference, the amount due, and the address the
   confirmation has been emailed to, and **View your booking** opens the booking page. That
   email carries the QR code.

The stub also says when the hold ends, in the booking policy's own words: an unpaid booking is
released {{ HOLD_RELEASE_MINUTES_BEFORE }} minutes before curtain, and the seats go back on sale.
A reminder is emailed {{ HOLD_REMINDER_MINUTES_BEFORE }} minutes before that happens. Online
booking stops at the same moment, or earlier where the performance has its own booking window,
and from then What's on and the show page say "Booking closed" for that night.

## Access and companion tickets

A signed-in member with a verified access profile sees a banner at the top of the form saying
how many access and companion tickets are still available to them for this performance, and the
Access and Companion ticket types cannot be set higher than that. Without a verified profile
those types do not appear. Recording the profile is on [Access requirements](/docs/my-nnt/access-requirements).

## Using a pass

A signed-in member holding an active pass that covers the performance sees **Your pass** above
the ticket lines, naming the pass and its reference, with **Use my pass**. Redeeming it is a
separate action from choosing tickets: it makes a zero-value booking of its own, with its own
reference and QR code, and nothing is due. A pass admits once per performance. The rest is on
[Passes](/docs/my-nnt/passes).

## What happens next

The booking is written with its tickets at the price shown, status unpaid, and no money moves.
The confirmation email carries the reference, the amount due, and the QR code the door reads.
The same QR code stays valid through any change to the booking. On the night, the desk finds
the booking by reference, name or scan and takes the payment; see [Desk](/docs/box-office/desk).
An unpaid booking whose hold passes is released as online booking stops, so its seats are sold
on the door rather than offered to the waiting list.
