Everybody signs in on the same screen, reached from **Sign in** at the top right of any public
page or by following a link to something that needs an account. If you were sent to it from a
member screen, you go back to that screen once you are in. There are two kinds of account, and
the screen says so at the top: committee addresses on `@newtheatre.org.uk` sign in with Google
and hold no password; everyone else uses an email address and a password, a passkey or an
emailed link.

::callout{icon="i-lucide-info" color="info"}
Already signed in? The sign-in and registration screens send you straight back to the site. A
second set of details typed here would make a second account by mistake. Sign out first from
the account menu if you want to come in as somebody else.
::

## If something goes wrong

- **"Those details do not match an account"**: the address or the password is wrong, the
  address has not been confirmed yet, or the account has been closed. The wording is the same
  for all four, so the form tells nobody who has an account. Try **I did not get my
  confirmation email** if you registered recently.
- **"That address signs in with Google"**: a committee address was typed into the password form.
  Use **Sign in with Google**.
- **"Only @newtheatre.org.uk accounts sign in with Google. Members use an email address and
  password."**: the Google account chosen is not a theatre one. Go back and use the form.
- **"That Google identity is already on another account. Those two need merging first."**: ask
  the IT Manager, who can merge the two from the console.
- **"That account cannot sign in. Ask the IT Manager."**: the account has been disabled or
  closed.
- **"Google sign-in is unavailable at the moment."**: Google refused the round trip. Try again in
  a minute; if it persists, tell the IT Manager.
- **"That code did not match"**: the six digits were wrong or already used. Wait for the app to
  show the next code and type that.
- **"That took too long. Sign in again."**: the code step timed out. Start from the password.
- **"That link has expired or has already been used. Ask for a new one."**: every emailed link
  works once and for a fixed time. **Ask for a new one** opens the sign-in screen already on the
  step that sends another, and the confirmation screen offers the form there and then.
- **"That link was for a different address. Ask for a new one."**: your address was changed
  again after the message was sent. The newest message is the one that works.
- **"That device did not check it was you. Turn on a device PIN or biometric and try again."**:
  the passkey was used without the device unlocking it first.
- **"That passkey is not on any account here"**: the passkey was made for a different site, or
  for an account since closed.
- **"Too many attempts. Try again shortly."**: the address has hit its sign-in limit for the
  window. Wait, then try once more.

## Signing in as a committee member

1. Press **Sign in with Google** and choose your `@newtheatre.org.uk` account.
2. If an account already exists on your address without a Google identity, the first Google
   sign-in claims it, with your bookings and roles already on it. Otherwise one is made for you,
   already confirmed.
3. You land back where you started.

A Workspace address never has a password. The forgotten-password and sign-in-link forms answer
one with "Theatre addresses sign in with Google; there is no password to reset", and the
password form refuses it with "That address signs in with Google".

## Signing in with a password

1. Type your email address and password and press **Sign in**.
2. If an authenticator app protects the account, the screen asks for the six digits it shows.
   Type them and you are in. A wrong code costs only the code: the screen stays put and asks
   again, and you do not go back to the password step.
3. If you do not have the app with you, press **I do not have my authenticator** and type one
   of the recovery codes you saved when you set it up. Each one works once. **Use my
   authenticator instead** puts the six-digit box back if you find your phone after all.

The code step waits {{ MFA_ATTEMPT_MINUTES }}; after that the screen says "That took too long.
Sign in again." Sign-in attempts are limited per address over a short window, counted on the
address typed whether or not it has an account, so being limited never proves one exists.

## Signing in with a passkey

Where the browser supports it, **Use a passkey** appears under the form. A passkey is the
device's own check that it is you (a fingerprint, a face or a PIN), so it stands for both steps:
no authenticator code follows it. Add one from [your account](/docs/getting-started/your-account)
once you are in.

## Signing in with an emailed link

1. Press **I have forgotten my password**, then **Email me a sign-in link instead**.
2. Type your address and press **Send a sign-in link**. The screen says "Check your email"
   whatever you typed, so it cannot be used to find out who has an account.
3. Open the link in the message. It signs you in and takes you where you were heading, or to the
   home page if you started from nowhere in particular. If an authenticator app protects the
   account, the six-digit code is still asked for: a link replaces the password, never the second
   step.

A sign-in link lasts {{ MAGIC_LINK_MINUTES }} and works once. Using it also confirms your
address, since opening the message proves the mailbox is yours.

## A forgotten password

1. Press **I have forgotten my password**, type your address and press **Send a reset link**.
   The screen says "Check your email" whatever you typed.
2. Open the link in the message. It lasts {{ PASSWORD_RESET_HOURS }} and works once.
3. Choose a new password of at least {{ PASSWORD_MIN_LENGTH }} characters and press **Set my
   password**. The hint under the field names every rule in force, so follow what it says there.
4. The screen says "Password set". Every other session on your account has ended, so any other
   device you were signed in on needs the new password too.

The same screen sets a first password when the theatre made your account for you, or when you
registered on an address that had already booked as a guest: the message you were sent opens it
with the heading "Choose your password", and that link lasts {{ ADMIN_TOKEN_HOURS }}.

## Creating an account

1. Press **I do not have an account yet**, or go to `/register`.
2. Give your name, your email address and a password. The hint under the field names the length
   and anything else the rule asks for. Then press **Create my account**.
3. The screen says "Check your email". Registering does not sign you in: follow the link in the
   message to confirm your address, and then sign in.
4. The confirmation screen says "Address confirmed" and offers **Sign in**.

If the address already has an account, the screen says the same thing and the message you receive
says so instead, so nobody can use the form to check whether somebody is a member. Where that
account has no way to sign in yet (a guest booking, or a row the import brought over), the
message is a claim link rather than "you already have an account": signing in as usual would
be a dead end. An address that no message could reach gets the same answer and no account. An account whose
address is never confirmed is tidied away after a while, so confirm it when the message arrives.

::callout{icon="i-lucide-lightbulb" color="info"}
Bought tickets as a guest before you had an account? An account with your bookings on it already
exists, without a password. Registering with that address sends you a message headed "Finish
setting up your account" with a link to choose one: follow it and the account is yours, bookings
and all. The link lasts {{ ADMIN_TOKEN_HOURS }}. Nothing is lost and nothing is duplicated, and
the screen says the same "Check your email" it says to everybody.
::

## Questions people ask

::accordion

::accordion-item{label="I have lost my phone and cannot get an authenticator code"}
At the code step press **I do not have my authenticator** and type one of the recovery codes you
saved when you set the app up; each works once. A passkey on another device also gets you in
without a code. If you have no codes left, the IT Manager resets the second factor on your
account, after which you sign in with your password alone and set the app up again.
::

::accordion-item{label="I have a passkey but no password, and the forgotten-password form sends me nothing"}
A reset link only goes to an account that has a password to reset, so a passkey-only or
link-only account sees the same "Check your email" and no message arrives. Sign in with the
passkey or an emailed link, then add a password under **Security**. A committee address holds no
password at all and sees a line saying so where the field would be.
::

::accordion-item{label="I registered but never received the confirmation email"}
Press **I did not get my confirmation email** and type the address. The answer on screen is the
same whatever you type; if the address is waiting to be confirmed, a fresh link is on its way.
Check the address you typed when you registered, since one no message could reach was never
given an account. Repeated requests for the same address are turned down for a short while.
::

::accordion-item{label="A sign-in method came off my account and it was not me"}
Removing a method sends a message to the account's address, so you would have heard, and every
change to how you sign in is in the audit trail with who made it. If it was not you, change your
password from the forgotten-password link, which signs out every other device, and tell the IT
Manager.
::

::accordion-item{label="Can I move my account to my theatre email address?"}
Only if the account holds no password. A committee address signs in with Google and can never
hold a password, so the change is refused on a password account. Ask the IT Manager, who merges
the two accounts; signing in with Google on an address that already has a password-less account
claims it with everything on it.
::

::

## What happens next

Every sign-in, and which method proved it, is written to the audit trail against your account.
Setting a new password from a reset link signs you out everywhere else; a plain sign-in does
not. Signing out from the account menu ends the session on that device only.
