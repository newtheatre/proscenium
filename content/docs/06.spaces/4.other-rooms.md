Other rooms is the catalogue of rooms we do not manage and cannot promise: the Students'
Union's rooms across campus. It is at **Manage, Spaces, Other rooms**. Seeing it needs
`rooms.read`; listing a room, editing one or noting what it is like needs `rooms.write`.

::callout{icon="i-lucide-info" color="info"}
**Nothing here holds a slot or appears on a calendar.** The catalogue is what a member may
state a preference for when they ask for a room we do not manage, and what we have learned
about each room, so nobody is sent to a meeting room to rehearse in again. The SU still
decides which room we get.
::

## The catalogue

![The catalogue with the List a room button (1), the table (2), a row's Note something button (3) and its Edit button (4)](/images/docs/spaces/other-rooms.png)

1. **List a room** adds one.
2. **The table** shows each room with its building and campus (or **Somewhere on campus**),
   how many it holds, and **Notes**: one badge per purpose, **Fine for**, **May not
   suit** or **No good for**, with the reason beside it and a cross to forget it. Search
   matches a room, a building or a campus. Retired rooms are hidden until the **Still worth
   asking for** filter asks for them.
3. **Note something** records a verdict about the room for one purpose.
4. **Edit** changes the room's details or retires it.

## Listing a room

We are told little about these rooms, so most of the form is optional.

1. Press **List a room**.
2. **Name** is required and must be unique: what everybody calls it, so somebody searching
   finds it.
3. **Building** and **Campus** say where it is; **How many it holds** is the capacity if you
   know it; **Who to ask** is usually a desk rather than a person.
4. **Still worth asking for** is on for a new room. Switch it off to retire one: a retired
   room stays on old requests but is not offered to anybody.
5. Press **List the room**, or **Save the room** when editing.

## Noting what a room is like

A verdict is recorded against one purpose. A fixed table ruins a rehearsal and is exactly
what a meeting wants, so the same room can be fine for one and no good for the other.

1. Press **Note something** on the row.

![The note form with the purpose buttons (1), the verdict buttons (2), the reason (3) and the Add the note button (4)](/images/docs/spaces/other-rooms-note.png)

2. **For what**: choose the purpose. The list is the one the configuration allows a room to
   be booked for.
3. **How it went**: **Fine for**, **May not suit** or **No good for**.
4. **Why** is required and is shown to anybody asking for that room for that purpose, so
   write it for them. The form says how many characters it takes.
5. Press **Add the note**. There is one verdict per room per purpose, so noting the same purpose
   again replaces what was there. The cross beside a badge forgets it, asking first: press
   **Forget the note** in the dialogue, or **Back**.

What a note does:

- A member choosing that room for that purpose on their form is warned before they ask.
- The request queue shows the warning against the room the member preferred.
- Recording a room marked **No good for** as the one the SU gave us is refused until the
  officer ticks **Record the room anyway**; **May not suit** warns and lets it through.
- **Send back** on the queue writes a note in the same action, so the catalogue builds itself
  out of the work rather than being a chore somebody remembers afterwards.

## If something goes wrong

- **"A room with that name is already listed"**: two rooms called the same thing is a
  catalogue nobody trusts. Find the existing one and edit it.
- **"Give the room a name people would recognise"**: the name is blank.
- **"Say what is wrong with it"**: a note with no reason.
- **"That room is no longer here."**, **"That note is no longer here."**: somebody removed it first. Reload.
- **"The rooms could not be read."**: the list did not load. Reload, and if it keeps
  happening say so.

## What happens next

Listing, editing and retiring a room are in the audit trail by name. A note is recorded with
the room, the purpose and the verdict, never its wording, because a reason may describe a
person's experience. Notes are what we believe about a room today rather than a permanent
record: a room that has its table taken out should stop being marked bad, so a note is
replaced or forgotten rather than superseded.
