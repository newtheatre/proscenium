Every console screen and every show-night screen carries a megaphone button beside the help
link. It opens a short form for telling the IT Manager that something is wrong, or that
something would help. It takes two taps and a sentence, and it works from the screen you are
on, so the report already says which screen you meant.

::callout{icon="i-lucide-info" color="info"}
**It is not for emergencies.** If the whole site is down, the IT Manager is already being told
by the health check. If somebody is hurt or a licensing question is live, use the incident log
on the show-night screens. The megaphone is for a screen that misbehaves, a step that is
awkward, or an idea worth writing down while you have it.
::

## If something goes wrong

- **"Too many attempts. Try again shortly."**: you have sent ten reports in the last hour.
  Wait a while, or put several things in one report.
- **"That is too short"**: a report needs at least ten characters. Say what you did, what you
  expected and what happened instead.
- **The button is not there**: you are on a page outside the console and show-night screens,
  such as the documentation or your own member pages, or your sign-in has ended. Sign in and
  open a console or show-night screen.

## Sending a report

1. Tap the megaphone beside the help link.
2. Choose **Something is wrong** or **An idea**.
3. Write what happened, or what would help. For a problem, the most useful report says three
   things: what you did, what you expected, and what happened instead. "The till froze after
   the second scan and the total did not update" finds a fault; "the till is broken" does not.
4. Tap **Send**. A message says **Thanks, we have it**, and that is the end of your part.

## What is attached for you

The report carries the address of the screen you were on, whether you were in the console or on
the show-night screens, the name and version of your browser, and the last few requests
your browser saw fail, each with the reference number the hosting provider stamps on it. Those
numbers let a fault be found in the server's own logs without anybody having to reproduce it.
Nothing else is taken: no screenshot, no form contents, nothing you did not type into the box.

## What happens next

Once a day, every new report is read and turned into an entry on the theatre's issue tracker.
A problem report is looked up in the server's logs from the minutes around it, and the entry
carries what was found and a first guess at the cause. An idea is written up as a draft story
in the shape the backlog uses, for the IT Manager to accept or not. Your words are quoted as
you wrote them; your name is not put on the tracker.

You are not told the outcome automatically. The IT Manager reads the tracker and will tell you
if they need more, or when the fix has landed. If something has gone quiet, ask them.

## Your report and your data

A report is kept as long as your account is. If your account is erased, the words you wrote
and the browser details are cleared and the report survives only as a count: one report, from
one screen, on one day. The tracker entry, which never carried your name, stays.
