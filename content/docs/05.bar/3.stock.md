The stock register lists what the bar counts: a bottle of gin in millilitres, a case of lager
as whole items, the crisps. It is at **Manage, Bar, Stock** and needs `bar.read` to open and
`bar.write` to change, both held by the Bar manager role. What is on hand is never typed in here:
it is the sum of the movements recorded against each item, and this screen is where a delivery,
wastage or an adjustment is recorded.

::callout{icon="i-lucide-info" color="info"}
**On hand is the sum of the movements.** Nothing stores a stock figure. Every quantity here is
added up from the movement history, so a mistake is corrected with a reversing movement and the
original stays where it is.
::

## Adding a stocked item

1. Press **Add a stocked item**.
2. Give it a **Name**: what the cellar list calls it. House red 750ml, Lager keg, Crisps.
3. Choose what it is **Counted in**: millilitres for anything poured, whole items for anything
   counted. This is fixed once stock has moved against the item.
4. For anything poured, optionally give the **Container size** in millilitres, so a delivery can
   be counted in bottles or kegs. Fixed once stock has moved, like the unit.
5. Optionally set a **Par level**, what the bar wants on hand before a show week. An item without
   one is left out of the order list.
6. Optionally give it a **Stock group**, free text for grouping the order list. This is not one of
   the till's own categories.
7. Optionally write **Allergen notes**, the reference a product's own note is written from.
8. Leave **Age restricted** on, which is where a new stocked item starts, for anything alcoholic; a product made of it should be restricted
   too.
9. Press **Add an item**.

![The stock register with the add button (1), the poured by column (2), the record a movement button (3), the edit button (4) and the retire button (5)](/images/docs/bar/stock.png)

1. **Add a stocked item** opens the form above.
2. **Poured by** names the products on the till that deplete the item, each a link to its serving
   sizes.
3. **Record a movement** opens the form for a delivery, wastage or an adjustment against this
   item. It appears only on an item that is not retired.
4. **Edit** changes the name, par level, category, allergen notes and age restriction. The unit
   and container size are locked once the item has movements.
5. **Retire** takes the item off the lists, asking first: press **Retire Coke 330ml** in the
   dialogue, or **Back**. **Bring back** restores it and asks nothing.

Each row shows what is **On hand**, the **Par level** if one is set, and a **Status**: *Out*
when nothing is on hand, *Below par* or *OK* against the par level, and no status at all for an
item with no par level and something on hand. **Poured by** names the products on the till that
deplete the item, each one a link to its serving sizes; a mixer counts the product that offers
the choice. It is read from the recipes every time the list is drawn, so it is never out of date,
and *Nothing on the till* means no live product pours it. The toolbar searches by name, filters by whether an
item is retired, and sorts by status or name.

## Recording a movement

A movement is written once and never edited. Correct one from the movement history instead.

1. Press **Record a movement** on the item.
2. Choose **What happened**: a **Delivery**, **Wastage** or an **Adjustment**. A reversal is not
   offered here; it is raised from the movement history against the movement it cancels.
3. For an adjustment, choose **Which way**: *Add to stock* or *Take off stock*. Stock is found as
   often as it is lost. A delivery only adds and wastage only takes away, so neither asks.
4. Enter the **Quantity** in the item's own unit. The form states whether it adds to or comes
   off what is on hand, so nobody types a minus sign.
5. For a delivery of something measured in millilitres with a container size set, enter **Cost a
   container** in pounds, for what the whole bottle or keg actually cost; the screen divides it
   to a cost per ml. Anything else asks **Cost a unit** in pounds directly. This is what gross
   profit is measured against, and a delivery whose cost nobody enters records none rather than
   0.
6. For wastage or an adjustment, choose a **Reason** from the list, which changes with **What
   happened**: wastage offers breakage, spillage, out of date, line cleaning, quality, training
   or other; an adjustment offers count correction, opening balance or other. It is a list
   rather than free text so waste can be reported on, and one reason cannot be mistaken for the
   other's.
7. Press **Record the movement**. On hand has moved with it.

A sale, a comp and a stocktake adjustment are written by the till and the stocktake screen, never
by hand here.

## Retiring and deleting an item

**Retire** asks first, and its dialogue is where a refusal is said. It is refused while the item
still has stock on hand: write it off as wastage or count it out in a stocktake first. Retiring
leaves every movement exactly where it is, and a retired item cannot have stock moved against it
until it is **Brought back**. A retired item also cannot be poured
from, so a product whose recipe calls for it cannot go on the till.

It is refused too while anything on the till still pours it, and the refusal names those
products rather than counting them. **Retire it and hide them** is the answer: the item retires
and every product pouring it comes off the till in one write, so nothing is ever left selling
something the bar no longer stocks. Their sizes, recipes, prices and history are untouched, and
each one records the change in the audit trail. Putting the item back is a separate decision, and
so is putting those products back on the till.

**Delete**, under **More actions** at the end of the row, appears only on an item nothing has
ever moved against. Once it has a single movement, it can only be retired.

## If something goes wrong

- **"still has stock on hand: write it off or count it out before retiring it"**: record the
  remaining quantity as wastage, or count it as 0 in a stocktake and apply it, then retire.
- **"is retired: put it back before moving stock against it"**: a movement against a retired
  item is refused. Put the item back first.
- **"has stock movements, so its unit and container size are fixed: retire it and add it
  again"**: the unit is what every movement is stated in and cannot change under them.
- **"has stock movements, so it can only be retired: every movement still has to resolve"**: the
  delete is refused because the history points at the item.
- **"is poured by ...: change those recipes, or retire it and hide them together"**: a product on
  the till still depletes the item. Change its recipe, or answer the refusal with **Retire it and
  hide them**.
- **"A stocked item is already called ..."**: names are unique, ignoring case. Choose another.
- **"needs a reason"**: wastage and an adjustment always carry one.
- **"changed while you were editing it"**: somebody else changed the item's status first. Reload
  and look again.

## What happens next

Each movement recorded here is a row in the append-only movement history, stamped with who
recorded it and when, and on hand across every screen moves with it at once. Adding, editing,
retiring or deleting an item is written to the audit trail with what changed. An item that drops
below its par level appears on the order list the next time it is opened.
