The bar reports read the ledger and the movement history for a period and show what sold, what
it cost, what the stocktakes found, and what was given away. They are at **Manage, Bar,
Reports**, and are read by the Bar Manager, the Treasurer and the IT Manager. The Treasurer reads them without seeing the catalogue or the stock register.

::callout{icon="i-lucide-info" color="info"}
**Nothing here is a stored total.** Every figure is queried fresh from the ledger and the
movement history when the screen asks, so a correction, a reversal or a late stocktake shows
the moment it lands.
::

## If something goes wrong

- **"A custom range must end after it starts"**: the *to* date is before the *from* date. Swap
  them.
- **"Give the date as YYYY-MM-DD"**: the date field was cleared or typed in another shape. Pick
  the date again.
- **"That is not a day on the calendar"**: the date has the right shape but names no day, such as
  a 31st in a thirty-day month. Pick the date again.
- **The report could not be read**: the sign-in has lapsed or the account holds neither
  the bar nor the ledger. Sign in again, or ask the IT Manager about the role.

## Choosing a period

![The reports screen with the period kind (1), the range fields (2), the refresh button (3), an export button (4) and the gross profit summary (5)](/images/docs/bar/reports.png)

1. **Period**: Night, Week, Year or Custom range.
2. **The dates** the kind asks for, each its own labelled field. The screen opens on a custom
   range of today to today.
3. **Refresh** runs the report again; it also reruns whenever the period changes.
4. **Export CSV** on each section downloads that section for the same period.
5. **The gross profit summary**: revenue, cost and the difference.

Every period runs on the London calendar.

- **Night** is one show night: from 04:00 on the date chosen to 04:00 the next morning, so a
  sale after midnight belongs to the night it was part of.
- **Week** is the seven days starting on the day chosen.
- **Year** is the committee year chosen from the list, which reads as the two calendar years it
  spans, "2025/26": 1 August of the first to 31 July of the second.
- **Custom range** is from the first date to the last, both included.

## The sections

- **Sales**: one row per category, product and serving size sold, with the quantity and the
  revenue.
- **Gross profit**: revenue against the delivered cost of what was depleted. The summary line
  gives revenue, cost and gross profit; the table beneath shows each stocked item, the quantity
  depleted and its cost, priced from the deliveries recorded on the stock register: a bottle's
  price is divided by what it held only when the report reads it, and each figure is rounded to
  the penny once. Stock poured for a comp counts on the cost side: it left the shelf like any
  other. A delivery that has been reversed leaves the costing, and an item whose deliveries carry
  no cost contributes nothing to the cost side. Both halves run on when the sale happened, so a sale recorded late keeps its
  takings and its cost in the same period; a voided charge credits back both its takings and its
  cost in the period it was voided in, the way the ledger treats every correction.
- **Stocktake variance**: for every stocktake applied in the period, each item's quantity
  variance and its value, costed the same way as gross profit. Only a counted item whose count
  differed from expected has a row: a count that agreed posts no movement and so has nothing to
  show.
- **Comps**: each comp given on the till, with its reason, who approved it and the value
  forgone. Comps given at the box office belong to the box office's own reports, not this one.
- **Discounts**: each discount, its percentage, how many times it was applied and what was given
  away.
- **Wastage**: what was written off in the period, grouped by reason, item and category, in the
  item's own unit and at cost. The reasons come from a fixed list, so a year adds up. A write-off
  entered as an adjustment counts too, as long as its reason is a loss: a count correction and an
  opening balance are not losses and never appear here. A movement that has been reversed is not
  in it either: as far as the bar is concerned it did not happen.

## When a section is longer than the screen

Comps and stocktake variance are one row per comp and per adjusted count, so a year has more of
them than any screen should show at once. Both sections show the first 25 and say so underneath:
*Showing 1 to 25 of 212. Narrow the period to see the rest.* Nothing is cut off silently; the
figure after **of** is the whole period. Choose a shorter period, or export.

## Exporting

1. Choose the period.
2. Press **Export CSV** on the section you want.
3. The file opens in a new tab or downloads. Money is formatted as pounds in the file; the
   comps and variance exports also carry when each row happened.

Sales, gross profit, discounts and wastage export whole. Comps and variance export 1000 rows at a time,
and when there is more than one page the file is named for the page it holds, for example
`bar-comps-report-page-1-of-3.csv`. The next page is the same link with `&page=2` on the end.

The export is guarded against a spreadsheet treating a cell as a formula, so a product name
beginning with an equals sign arrives as text. A figure is left alone: a negative variance or a
negative on-hand arrives as a number the spreadsheet can total, not as text.

## What happens next

Reading a report writes nothing. Each export is recorded in the audit trail with the section and
the period it covered, never the figures.
