A stocktake is a count of the bar against what the movement history says should be there. It is
at **Manage, Bar, Stocktakes**, and the Bar Manager reads past stocktakes and opens a new one,
counts into it and applies it. The variance is
never hidden and never corrected on its own: it is shown as it is counted, and written to stock
only when somebody deliberately applies it.

The first stocktake ever applied is the bar's opening balance. Until it is, the till labels a
size, or a mixer in a size's choice, with nothing on hand **Out of stock** but still lets it be
pressed; from then on, the button is greyed out too (see [Till](/docs/tonight/till#out-of-stock)).

::callout{icon="i-lucide-info" color="info"}
**A blank count and a count of 0 are different things.** An item you did not count stays
blank and posts nothing when the stocktake is applied. Only a figure you typed, including 0,
counts.
::

## If something goes wrong

- **"This stocktake is no longer open, so nothing here was recorded"**: somebody applied it
  while you were counting. The line that says so was not saved; open a new stocktake and count
  again.
- **"Not saved: ..."** under a line: that one count did not reach the register, and the footer
  counts how many are in that state. Type it again, or press **Apply**, which tries every unsaved
  line again before it asks anything and stops if any still fails.
- **"This stocktake has already been applied"**: somebody applied it first. Reload and view
  the result.
- **"Joined the stocktake already open"**: not a refusal. Only one stocktake is open at a time,
  so you are counting into the one that already exists.
- **"That did not save. Try again, and tell the IT Manager if it keeps happening."**: the screen
  tried three times and could not claim the open slot. Try again; if it repeats, report the page.

## Opening a stocktake

1. Press **Open a stocktake**.
2. The screen opens the new stocktake. If one is already open, you join it instead, and the
   message says so: there is one open stocktake at a time.

Opening captures the expected on-hand figure for every stocked item that is not retired, at that
moment, so a sale after this cannot muddy the comparison.

![The stocktake list with the open button (1) and the count or view link (2)](/images/docs/bar/stocktakes.png)

1. **Open a stocktake** starts a new count, or joins the one already open.
2. **Count** opens an open stocktake; **View** opens an applied one.

The list shows each stocktake's status, **Open** or **Applied**, when it was opened and when it
was applied. The toolbar searches and filters on the status and sorts by when it was opened,
newest first.

## Counting

![One stocktake with the uncounted total (1), the lines grouped by stock group (2) and a variance (3)](/images/docs/bar/stocktake.png)

1. **How many items are not yet counted**, beside when the stocktake was opened.
2. **The lines**, one per stocked item, under a heading for each stock group (the **Stock group**
   set on the stock register), so the count walks the bar shelf by shelf. Items with no group
   come last, under *No stock group*.
3. **Variance** is the counted figure minus what was expected when the stocktake opened, in the
   item's unit, with its cost beside it once the count has saved.

Counting is built for a phone in one hand, over as many visits as it takes.

1. Walk the bar and type each item's count. Anything measured in millilitres with a container
   size asks two things: how many **Full** bottles or kegs there are, and how many millilitres are
   left in the **Open** one. Three full bottles and half of a fourth 750 ml bottle is *3* and
   *375*, and the line reads it back both ways (*3 full and 375 ml open, 2625 ml*), so nobody
   works the millilitres out by hand. Anything else asks one figure in its own unit.
2. Each count saves as soon as you leave the field or press Enter, and the line says **Saved**. A
   phone that sleeps, or a page that closes, loses nothing already typed. Enter also moves on: from
   **Full** to **Open**, then to the next item.
3. What the line was **Expected** to hold stays hidden until it is counted, so the count is what
   is on the shelf rather than what the screen said it should be.
4. The footer stays at the bottom of the screen: how many lines are counted, **Only uncounted** to
   hide every line already counted, and **Apply**.
5. Leave an item blank if you did not count it; clearing both fields puts it back to blank.

## Applying

1. Press **Apply**. Any line still saving is waited for, and any that failed is tried again, so
   the confirmation reads what the register actually holds.
2. The confirmation names how many items are counted, how many are not, and the net variance at
   cost about to be posted. Press **Apply** again to go ahead.
3. One adjustment movement is posted for every counted item whose count differs from what was
   expected, all in one step, and the stocktake is frozen for good.

A blank line writes nothing and shows as *Uncounted* afterwards. A counted item that matched its
expected figure writes nothing either. A frozen stocktake is never edited: a mistake is corrected
by a new stocktake or a reversing movement.

## What happens next

Opening, every saved count and the apply are written to the audit trail; the trail records how
many lines a save touched, never the figures. Applying posts the adjustments to the movement
history as **Stocktake adjustment** rows pointing at their stocktake lines, so on hand moves at
once on the stock register and the order list, and the variance appears on the reports for the
period the stocktake was applied in.
