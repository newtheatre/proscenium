A show is the production; a performance is one dated, timed slot of it in one venue. Both are
managed from **Manage, Box office, Shows** by the Front of House Manager, who reads the list and
makes every change to it.

::callout{icon="i-lucide-info" color="info"}
**Nothing goes on sale by accident.** A show is a draft, invisible to the public, until you
publish it. A performance is off sale until you put it on sale, or publish its show with the
cascade on. Nothing here is operational: it can be done weeks ahead, at a desk.
::

## If something goes wrong

- **"A show already has the address /shows/hamlet"**: two shows cannot share an address. Add a
  year or the venue to it.
- **"The season of Hamlet changed to Autumn 2026 while you were editing it"**: somebody else set
  it, or adding the first performance did, since the Details tab opened. Nothing was saved.
  Reload, then choose the season again if it is still wrong. A save that leaves the season as it
  was keeps the one set since, and saves everything else.
- **"Season pass is not on sale for Hamlet's dates, so it cannot cover it. Nothing has been
  published."**: the pass has been taken off sale, or its dates no longer hold one of the show's
  performances. The dialogue closes; press **Publish** again, and it offers only the passes on
  sale now.
- **"Hamlet cannot publish: its category, Drama, has been retired"** (or its season): pick a
  current one on the Details tab and save, then publish.
- **"Drama is retired and cannot be chosen for a show"**: the category or season picked on the
  Details tab was retired since the page loaded. Nothing was saved; pick a current one. One the
  show already carries stays through any save.
- **"Hamlet has sold 40 tickets, so it cannot be deleted: unpublish it, and cancel the
  performances that are not going ahead"**: sold history is never destroyed.
- **"40 tickets have been sold for this performance, so it can only be cancelled"** or
  **"3 shifts on this performance have been taken, so it can only be cancelled"**: use
  **Cancel** instead, and tell the people it names.
- **"This performance has been cancelled, so it cannot be put on sale"**: a cancelled
  performance stays cancelled; add a new one.
- **"Tickets for this performance are sold at https://..., so it cannot go on sale here"**:
  clear the external link first, then use **On sale**.
- **"Studio is retired and cannot be booked for a new performance"**: bring the venue back
  on [Venues](/docs/box-office/venues), or choose another.
- A capacity refusal quoting both figures: the capacity being set is below the seats already
  sold. Lower it no further than the sold figure, or cancel instead.
- **"Doors open before curtain, not after it"**: the doors time is later than the curtain time.
  Only a curtain after midnight and before 04:00 may have later-looking doors, which fall on the
  evening before.
- **"A performance at Studio needs its running time: every shift on its rota ends from it"**:
  give the running time in minutes, without the intervals. Every volunteer's shift ends a set
  time after the show does, so without it the whole rota would be sent home soon after curtain.
  Only a performance at an external venue, or a cancelled one, may leave it empty.
- **"Every content warning needs a grade before this can be saved"**: grade the warnings the
  notice names, or remove them.
- **"A show either has no warnings or has these ones; it cannot be confirmed clear and warned
  at once"**: turn the switch off, or clear the warnings.
- **"That is not an image we can take. Posters are JPEG, PNG or WebP."**: convert the file
  and try again.
- **"This export would return more than 20,000 rows. Narrow the show, performance, date range
  or source and try again."**: the ticket sales export is capped. Choose a shorter range.

## The list

![The shows list with the season line (1), the search and filters (2), the Add a show button (3) and the table (4)](/images/docs/box-office/shows.png)

1. **The season line** under the heading counts the shows the filters show, how many are on
   sale and how many are drafts.
2. **Search** by title or web address, and filter by **Status**, **Season**, **Show category**,
   whether **Warnings** have been assessed, whether anything is **On sale**, and **Running time
   missing** (a performance still to come at a venue we run has none). The filters live in the
   address bar, so a filtered list can be bookmarked or sent.
3. **Add a show** asks only for a title and its web address (the public page is `/shows/` and
   the web address, lowercase words joined by hyphens, written from the title until you change
   it).
   It opens the new show's own screen, as a draft.
4. **The table** shows each show's dates, venues, standing (Draft, On sale, Off sale or
   Completed) and how much of its house is reserved. **Edit** opens it. A draft with no poster
   is named in an amber notice under the table, and so is a show with a performance still to
   come at a venue we run that has no running time. The diary imported from the old system
   left some of those: open each show and give every such performance its running time before
   its night. Saving it moves its shifts to end when the show does.

## The show screen

![A show with the status strip (1), the tabs (2), the poster (3), the publish checklist (4) and the danger zone (5)](/images/docs/box-office/show.png)

1. **The status strip** says at a glance whether anything is on sale, when the next performance
   is, how much of the house is sold, and how many tickets are unpaid and awaiting the desk.
2. **The tabs** are Details, Performances, Ticket types, Content warnings and Sales. The tab
   is in the address, so a link opens on the one it names. Opening another tab with unsaved
   Details on screen asks first: press **Discard the changes**, or **Back** to return to them.
3. **The poster** is uploaded, replaced or removed here: JPEG, PNG or WebP up to 5 MB. Until
   one is uploaded the listing and the show page draw the show's own gradient. Show art is
   sovereign: no house styling is ever applied to it.
4. **The publish checklist** reports whether a poster is uploaded, performances are scheduled,
   every performance still to come has its running time, every pass on sale for the show's dates
   covers it, and pricing is set. It gates nothing: an outstanding item never stops the publish
   button.
5. **The danger zone** holds **Take off sale** for a published show and **Delete this show**
   for one nothing has ever been sold under.

### Details

The **Basics** card carries the title, address, an optional tagline, the short description the
listing shows beside the poster, the full description, and the **Show category** and **Season**
pickers. A retired category or season is not offered for new work, but one this show already
carries stays in its picker. A show with no season takes the season a performance's night falls
in when that performance is added, unless the show already has an earlier live performance, and
the trail says so; once it has a season, no performance changes it, even one on an earlier night,
and a season picked here is never replaced. The **Show info card** carries age guidance, the latecomers policy
(admitted, admitted at the interval, not admitted, or not yet stated), and **Online booking
closes**, in hours before curtain, which every performance inherits unless it states its own;
empty means curtain-up. Content warnings are counted here with a link to assess them. Press
**Save the show**.

### Performances

1. Press **Add performances**. It is disabled until at least one venue exists that is not
   retired, and a notice beside it says so and links to Venues.
2. Choose the **Venue**. It starts at the one the show's latest performance was added at, or the
   first venue we run for a show's first performance. An external venue reads "(external: no
   rota)" in the list: a performance there gets no shifts, which are added by hand on the rota
   board.
3. Give the **Days**: one day, or press **Add another day** for each further night of the run
   (up to a month of nights at once; the cross beside a day removes it). Each is the day the
   curtain falls on: a 00:15 curtain early on Saturday is given as the Saturday, and belongs to
   Friday's show night.
4. Give the **Curtain** time and, optionally, **Doors**, the same for every night. Times are
   London wall-clock times, each read on its own day, so a run across a clock change keeps its
   curtain. Doors open before the curtain: a doors time after it is refused, except for a curtain
   after midnight and before 04:00, whose doors are on the evening before.
5. Give the **Running time** in minutes, without the intervals, once for the run. It is required
   at a venue we run: every shift on the rota ends a set time after the show does. Only an
   external venue may leave it empty. Give the number of intervals and their length.
6. Press **Add the performance** (or **Add 3 performances**, and so on). Every night is added,
   or none is if one of them is refused. Each is off sale until you put it on sale, or publish
   the show.

The rare settings are not on this form. To give one performance a **Capacity** override (empty
takes the venue's; 0 is a closed house), an **Online booking closes** override, an **Unpaid
holds release** override in minutes before curtain (empty takes
{{ HOLD_RELEASE_MINUTES_BEFORE }}, the house default), an **External ticketing** link or
internal notes nobody outside the committee sees, press **Edit** on its row, then **More**.

Each row then offers **Edit**, **Prices** and **On sale** or **Off sale** in line, with
**More actions** at the end of it holding **Duplicate** (the form again, holding this
performance's venue, times and running time, for the next night), **Cancel the performance** and
**Delete** while nothing has been sold for it. Under its day the row names the venue and its
online booking window and where it came from ("from the show" when inherited), and **Sold** gives
the seats sold against the capacity ("12 of 120"). A
performance still to come at a venue we run with no running time carries an amber **No running
time** badge: press **Edit**, give it one and save. The list is searchable by venue and filtered
by status, venue, curtain day and whether the performance is ticketed elsewhere.

::callout{icon="i-lucide-triangle-alert" color="warning"}
**Cancelling** stops sales and keeps the performance on the record; everybody holding a ticket
has to be refunded at the desk and told, and the screen counts them. Cancelling does not email
ticket holders: tell them from [Announce](/docs/communications/announce) with **Ticket holders
for a performance**. **Deleting** is only for a
performance with no sales and no shifts taken; it cannot be undone.
::

### External ticketing

Set the **External ticketing** link on a performance and every internal sales path refuses it,
pointing at that link instead: the public page links out, the desk cannot sell it, and the
Sales tab reports it as externally ticketed with no house figure of ours. Clearing the link does
not put the performance back on sale; use **On sale** for that.

### Ticket types and prices

A price resolves this performance, then the show, then the ticket type's base price. The
**Ticket types** tab sets the show level; **Prices** on a performance row sets the performance
level, with the show and base figures shown beside it so you can see why a price is what it is.
Leave a field empty to inherit; an explicit 0 is a free ticket, not an absence. Each type
can also be **On sale**, **Off sale** or **Inherit** at each level. Press **Save prices**.
A change takes effect for new bookings only; every ticket already sold keeps the price it sold
at.

### Content warnings

A show warns from the vocabulary, never in its own words. Tick the **Staging** warnings the
production does to the room (not graded), pick the **Content** warnings it contains and grade
each **Mentioned**, **Discussed** or **Depicted** (**Set all to** grades them at once), add
optional notes for the show page, and press **Save warnings**. The button is disabled while any
content warning is ungraded. The switch **Assessed, and there is nothing to warn about** is its
own answer, distinct from nobody having looked: until one or the other is given, the show page
says the warnings have not been assessed. Choosing a warning withdraws the switch, and the other
way about.

### Sales

**How the run is selling** lists each performance with its capacity, seats sold, unpaid tickets
and seats left, with a total for the run. It is counted from the seats held right now, so a
released hold is back in the house here too. Unpaid is what the desk still has to take money
for.

## Publishing

1. Press **Publish** in the heading. The dialogue, **Put this show on sale**, offers **Put its
   performances on sale too**, on by default; a cancelled performance is never put back on sale
   with them.
2. It reads back what goes on sale, so a mistake shows before a booker sees it: each night with
   its venue, running time and intervals, and the shifts stamped for it ("No shifts stamped" at
   a venue we run is worth fixing first); the prices a booker pays for each ticket type; how many
   content warnings it carries, or whether it is assessed; and whether a poster is uploaded.
   Nothing here stops you.
3. Under **Passes on sale for its dates** the dialogue lists each pass on sale that is valid on
   one of the show's performances. Tick each pass that should cover the show. For a show playing
   only at venues we run, every pass starts ticked; with an external night, none does. A pass
   already covering the show is shown ticked and cannot be changed here. A holder of a pass that
   does not cover the show is refused at the door.
4. Press **Put on sale**. The public page goes live, each ticked pass covers the show, and
   the screen counts the performances put on sale. **Preview public page** then opens it in a new
   tab. Removing a show from a pass is still done on [Pass types](/docs/box-office/pass-types).

**Take off sale**, in the danger zone, closes sales and removes the public page. Press it, then
**Take Hamlet off sale** in the dialogue. Existing bookings are kept and nothing sold is touched.

## The ticket sales export

Under the table, **Ticket sales export** takes a copy of what has sold as a CSV, for reporting.
The Front of House Manager takes it; front of house crew are not shown the card at all.

1. Set the two days. They are the first and last day of sales to include, and the range is
   inclusive of both.
2. Press **Export CSV**. The browser saves the file.

One row per seat: the booking reference, the performance (show and start), the ticket type, the
price paid, where it was sold, whether it was collected and whether it has been refunded. No
customer name, no note and no access information is ever in it. Taking a copy is recorded in the
audit trail with your name, the range and the number of rows.

A range wide enough to return more than 20,000 rows is refused rather than quietly cut short;
narrow the days and export again.

## What happens next

Publishing makes the show's public page live and, with the cascade, puts its performances on
sale, so the what's-on listing shows them with honest availability: the listing says tickets are
limited once {{ LISTING_LIMITED_THRESHOLD_PERCENT }} or less of a house is left. A
cancelled performance refuses every sale from that moment and is counted for its refunds on the
desk. Every change to a show, a performance, a price or a warning is in the audit trail.
