The audit trail is the one record of every privileged action taken in the system: who did what,
to whom, when, and what changed. It is at **Manage, System, Audit trail** and needs
`audit.read`, which Administrators, Managers and the Theatre Manager hold; recording an entry by
hand needs `audit.write`, held by the same three. Nothing on the trail can be edited or deleted,
by anybody, ever: the database refuses it. A correction is a new entry.

::callout{icon="i-lucide-info" color="info"}
**The trail names people by reference, never by free text.** An entry carries identifiers and
structured values: the account it is about, the field that changed, the value before and after.
A note, a reason or a description about a person is never written into it, because an
append-only table cannot be scrubbed when that person asks to be erased. Erasure redacts the
identifying values in their old entries, and that redaction is the only edit the trail ever
accepts. The names you see on the screen are looked up as it renders, so an erased person reads
as their anonymised placeholder.
::

## Reading the trail

![The audit trail with the search field (1), the filters button (2), Record something (3), Export (4) and the entries (5)](/images/docs/system/audit.png)

1. **Search** matches who or what an entry is about.
2. **Filters** opens the filter panel, below. What is filtered shows as chips beneath the
   toolbar, each with its own clear button and a **Clear all**.
3. **Record something** opens the form for an entry about something that happened outside the
   system, below.
4. **Export** downloads the entries the current search and filters match, as a spreadsheet.
5. **The entries**, newest first. Each row has five columns.

- **When**: the date and time, London.
- **Who**: the person who acted. An entry the system wrote for itself (a nightly sweep, a
  scheduled export) shows a **System** badge instead of a name, so a blank never looks like
  missing data.
- **What**: the action in plain words, for example **Setting changed**, **Role granted** or
  **Restore drill recorded**. An entry recorded by hand carries a **Recorded by hand** badge.
- **To whom**: the person the entry is about, or the raw target when it is about something
  else (a setting, a show, a room).
- **What changed**: the field-by-field difference, `field: before → after`, plus any other
  structured detail. A wide change shows its first three parts and **+N more**; the arrow at the
  start of the row opens the rest.

On a phone the table becomes one card per entry with the same content.

## Filtering

Every filter, the sort and the page live in the address bar, so a view you have narrowed can be
sent to somebody as a link and comes back the same.

1. Press **Filters**.
2. Choose any of: **Actor** (a person), **Module** (the area of the system: identity, spaces,
   ticketing, show night, bar, training, communications, finance, governance), **Action** (one
   named action), **Recorded** (a date range).
3. Sort by **When** or by **Recorded order**, which breaks a tie between entries written in the
   same second.

The total beneath the table counts what matched, and the pages follow it.

## Exporting

**Export** writes the entries the current search and filters match to `audit-trail.csv`: the
id, when it occurred, the actor's id and name (or `system`), the action, the target and the
detail as JSON. An export is capped at 5,000 rows, newest first; narrow the filters to get
older ones. Taking a copy of the trail is itself an act on it, so every export writes an
**Audit trail exported** entry naming what was asked for and how many rows it returned.

## Recording something that happened outside the system

A decision the committee took in a meeting, or a change made in another system, can be put on
the trail after the fact so the record is complete. The entry is signed: it is written against
you, from a session that has answered a second factor, and everybody it names has to be an
account here, because a name typed in would be personal free text.

1. Press **Record something**.
2. Choose **What happened**. Only four things can be recorded by hand: a role granted or
   revoked outside the system, and an account disabled or enabled outside the system. There is
   no general-purpose entry, because that would be a note.
3. Pick **Who it was about**.
4. Pick **Who decided it**: whose decision this was, not yours. You are the signature.
5. Give **When it happened**, the real date rather than today.
6. Press **Sign and record it**.

If your session's second factor is older than the window allows, a re-authentication dialog
opens over the form; answer it and the entry is recorded without retyping anything.

## If something goes wrong

- **"A manual entry is signed, so it needs an authenticator on your account"**: your account
  has no second factor enrolled. **Set up an authenticator app** takes you to your security
  settings; enrol, then record the entry.
- **"That date has not happened yet"**: the date is in the future. Give the real date.
- **"The subject is not an account on this system"** or **"The person it is recorded for is not
  an account on this system"**: the person you picked has no account here. Somebody with no
  account cannot be named on the trail; create the account first if they should have one.
- **"A manual entry records a manual.* action"**: the action chosen is one the system performs
  itself. Choose one of the four hand-recorded actions.
- **"No entry matches that."**: the search or filters exclude everything. Clear a chip.
- **"Nothing on the trail yet. Every privileged action lands here."**: the trail is empty, which
  on a live system means the database has just been reset.

## What happens next

A hand-recorded entry lands on the trail at once, marked **Recorded by hand**, with you as the
signer and the real actor and date carried as structured fields beside your signature. An export
lands as an **Audit trail exported** entry against you. Neither can be edited afterwards.
