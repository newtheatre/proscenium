The catalogue is the list of everything the theatre teaches. It is at **Manage, Training,
Catalogue** and opens for anybody with the `training.read` permission (the Training Manager
and Manager roles) and for every current department lead, who sees only the departments they
lead. Adding or changing a module needs `training.write`, or a lead's standing in the module's
own department.

::callout{icon="i-lucide-info" color="info"}
A module declares its expiry policy once. Whether somebody currently holds it is worked out
from the dates on their record every time it is asked, so nothing on this screen has to be kept
up to date after a change in policy.
::

## Finding a module

![The catalogue with the search field (1), the filters (2), the Add a module button (3), the table (4) and a row's Edit button (5)](/images/docs/training/catalogue.png)

1. **Search** by a module id or its title.
2. **Filters** narrow the list by department, kind, status, whether it is safety critical, and
   how it is delivered; the sort is list order or title. The filters live in the address bar, so
   a filtered view can be bookmarked or sent to somebody.
3. **Add a module** is disabled until at least one department exists.
4. Each row shows the id, the title, a **Safety critical** badge where it applies, the
   department, kind and delivery mode; under **Lifetime**, the policy and what it would mean for
   a record earned today; under **Grants**, whether holding it makes somebody a trainer or a
   supervisor, and whether it is awarded by sign-off.
5. **Edit** opens the module.

## Adding a module

1. Press **Add a module**.
2. Give it a **Published id**: uppercase letters, digits and hyphens, in the style TECH-111 or
   FOH-101. This is what members quote and what a certificate carries, so it is fixed once the
   module exists.
3. Give it a **Title** and, optionally, **What it covers**. The description is shown to members
   in the catalogue, so write it for somebody deciding whether to take it.
4. Choose the **Department** it belongs to.
5. Choose the **Kind**: a **Module**, a **Certification** or a **Brief**. A brief is taught once:
   it carries no expiry, grants no standing and can never be a prerequisite. Only a
   certification can grant trainer or supervisor standing.
6. Choose **How it is delivered**: in person, self-directed or hybrid. A safety-critical module
   can never be fully self-directed, so that choice disappears when the switch is on.
7. Choose **How long it is worth**: never expires, a number of months from award, or ends with
   the academic year, which turns over on {{ ACADEMIC_YEAR_BOUNDARY }} (month and day). The
   policy is stamped on a record the day it is earned and never recomputed by a later change
   here.
8. Set the **Status**. A draft is invisible to members. Retiring a module blocks new sessions and
   sign-offs and leaves existing records readable.
9. Set the switches. **Safety critical** makes a missing prerequisite a hard block at sign-up
   and on the register rather than a warning. **Awarded by sign-off rather than by a register**
   means it cannot be taught by session. **An external certificate can satisfy it** lets a
   lead record outside evidence against it, and **Evidence we accept** names what that evidence
   is, so the person recording one knows whether a piece of paper counts.
10. **Where it sits in the list** orders the catalogue; lower comes first. **Notes for leads**
    are never shown to members.
11. Add **Material links**, each a label and a URL. Members see them only when signed in.
12. Press **Add the module**. A new module starts as a draft.

## Prerequisites

A module can declare what must be held before it. The **Needs first** field appears only when
editing a module that already exists.

1. Open the module and find **Needs first**.
2. Pick modules from the list. Each pick is saved as you make it, and the sentence beneath says
   what is now needed.
3. Untick one to withdraw it. Nothing about the records already earned under it changes: a
   record is what happened.

Only direct edges are declared; what those modules need in turn is followed automatically. A
brief never appears in the list, a module cannot require itself, and a pick that would close a
loop is refused with the path named.

## What is fixed once records exist

Once anybody holds a record against a module, its kind and the standing it grants are fixed:
the editor disables those fields and says so. An expired record still counts, because it was
awarded under those meanings. To mean something different, retire the module and create a
successor.

## The public catalogue

The catalogue itself is public at `/training/modules`, grouped by department, with what each
module needs first and its next open session. Drafts and retired modules never appear. Signing
in adds whether the reader holds each module and shows the material links; nothing else
changes. See [Your training](/docs/members/your-training).

## If something goes wrong

- **"Add a department first: every module belongs to one."**: the catalogue is empty and there
  is nowhere to put a module. Go to [Departments](/docs/training/departments).
- **"A module already has that id"**: the published id is taken. Ids are never reused.
- **"Records exist against this module, so its kind and the standing it grants are fixed."**:
  see above; retire it and make a successor.
- **"A brief cannot be a prerequisite: it gates nothing and never expires"**, **"A module cannot
  require itself"**, **"That module is already required"**: the pick was refused and the
  control put back.
- **"That would make ... a prerequisite of itself, through ..."**: the refusal names the chain of modules that would
  lead back to this one. Remove one of those edges first.
- **"... requires this module, so it cannot become a brief"**: another module depends on it.
  Withdraw that prerequisite before changing the kind.
- **"You do not have permission to do that"**: a lead is editing a module outside their own
  departments, or moving one into a department they do not lead.
- **"The catalogue could not be read."**: the list failed to load, which is not the same as
  the catalogue being empty. Reload.

## What happens next

A new module is a draft until its status is changed; an active one appears in the public
catalogue and can be taught by session or signed off. Creating and editing a module, and adding
or withdrawing a prerequisite, are each in the audit trail with the actor and the module.
