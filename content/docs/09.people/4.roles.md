A role is a standing grant for administrative work: the sit-down jobs done at a desk in the
weeks before a show. It is granted by the IT Manager and runs out at the end of the committee
year unless it is marked permanent. This page is the reference for what each role reaches and
how the register is worked. It is for the reader who has just taken over a role and wants to
know what it opens, and for the IT Manager working out which role somebody needs.

::callout{icon="i-lucide-info" color="info"}
**A role opens the console, not the night.** The door, the till and the duty manager's screen
open to whoever holds tonight's confirmed shift, and a trainer is anybody holding a current
record on a trainer-granting module. No role is a workaround for a missing shift or record: the
fix is the shift or the record.
::

## The roles

Each role is called the same thing wherever it is read: in the grant list, in a filter, in a
refusal and here.

| Role | What it opens |
| --- | --- |
| IT Manager | Everything, including everything below and what no other role opens: creating, disabling, merging and erasing accounts, granting and revoking roles, changing settings, backups and restore drills, revoking training records, announcements and the send log, reopening a closed period. |
| Manager | Reading accounts and the audit trail and recording an entry on it by hand, reading settings, the roll of Fellows and the membership register (reading and writing), approving refunds, the rooms and the room requests, narrowing a pass type with live passes, and the training catalogue (reading and writing). |
| Theatre Manager | Reading accounts, the audit trail (and recording on it what happened away from a screen), settings, the roll and the register; the rooms and the room requests; reading the training catalogue. |
| Training Manager | The training catalogue (reading and writing), appointing a department's leads, revoking a record, recording training for an address with no account yet; reading accounts, the register and the rooms. |
| Front of House Manager | The committee post that is also the Box Office Manager, so one role. What the programme is set up as: shows, performances, ticket types and their prices, the desk, and the ticket sales export. The rota and its templates, checklists, the emergency card, the backstage board's presets, exporting the Challenge 25 register, reports across seasons; and the one exception below, opening the door and the duty manager's screen without a shift, which on the night includes approving a refund for tonight's performance. |
| Front of House | Nothing standing. It marks the person as front of house crew for the rota, and everything they do on a night comes from a shift. |
| Bar Manager | The bar's catalogue and its stock register, and the one exception below, opening the till without a bar shift. |
| Accessibility Officer | Verifying access profile declarations: sighting evidence and agreeing the door's wording. Nothing else, not even general box office. |
| Safety Officer | Which incident severities reach them, reading and closing the open-items list, reports across seasons. |
| Treasurer | Reading the ledger and every report built on it, recording a Z reading and resolving a variance, and the SU accounting export. |
| Committee | The money dashboard's totals and reports across seasons, never the drill-down into one night. |

A role earns a permission when the thing it unlocks exists. Where a screen is missing from a
role's list, that is deliberate rather than an oversight, and the change is a decision for the
IT Manager, not a grant made in the meantime.

## The committee year

Every grant expires at the end of the committee year, **31 July, the last instant of the day in
London**, unless it was marked permanent when granted. The expiry is checked every time the
grant is used, not by a nightly job: a grant that lapsed overnight grants nothing from the next
request onwards, and revoking one takes effect the same way. So the handover needs nobody to
remember to revoke anything; what it needs is the incoming committee's grants to be made.

- Holders are warned {{ ROLE_LAPSE_NOTICE_DAYS }} days before a grant lapses, once per grant
  and date.
- The IT Manager receives a monthly digest of grants expiring, grants lapsed and the standing
  list of permanent grants, so the exceptions stay visible.
- A grant that lapsed more than {{ ROLE_GRANT_PRUNE_DAYS }} days ago is tidied away. The audit
  trail keeps the history; the row is what goes.

A permanent grant is the exception, made by choosing **Further notice** under **Until**, and it
should be rare: the IT Manager's own role is the usual case, to keep the theatre from being without
one on 1 August. Every permanent grant is listed on the register, across every role, so the
exceptions cannot quietly accumulate. One granted by address before a first sign-in waits under
**Waiting for a first sign-in** instead, and joins the permanent grants once the person signs in.

## The register

**People > Roles** is the register, and the place a handover is worked. It lists every role with
the number of people currently holding it; choosing one shows its holders, until when, who
granted it and any note that was written. The chosen role is part of the address, so a list can
be sent to somebody as a link.

- **Granting.** With a role chosen, search for the person, choose **Until** (the committee year
  end, a date you pick, or further notice) and write a note if the grant needs explaining. The
  note sits on the grant for whoever reads the register next; it is never copied into the audit
  trail, which records only that one was written.
- **Granting before a first sign-in.** Somebody incoming may have no account yet. When the search
  finds nobody, choose **Nobody found? Grant it by address before their first sign-in**, then give
  their address and name. The address is only offered once the search has come back empty: anybody
  who already has an account is chosen from the search, and typing their address is refused with
  that instruction. So is a theatre address already waiting to be linked to somebody's account:
  the refusal names that account, and that is the one to choose. The grant waits under **Waiting for a first sign-in**, apart from the holders
  and not counted among them, until the person signs in: a theatre address by Google, any other
  address through the link it is sent to set a password (or by registering, which sends the same
  kind of link). It carries the same expiry and note as any grant and can be revoked from that
  list before it is ever used. A waiting IT Manager does not count for the protected role below.
- **Renewing.** Granting a role somebody already has a row for renews that row: the expiry, the
  note and the provenance are replaced and the lapse warning is re-armed. The audit trail records
  it as a renewal, with the old expiry and the new one side by side.
- **Revoking.** Revoke sits beside each holder, and asks first: press **Revoke Treasurer** (the
  role's own name) in the dialogue, or **Back**. It takes effect on the next request, everywhere. A refusal, such as
  the last IT Manager, is said inside that dialogue.
- **Lapsed grants** are hidden until asked for, and the total line says how many were hidden.
  Choose **Show them**, or the **Lapsed** filter, to see them.

The same grants are reachable from a person's own page under
[Accounts](/docs/people/accounts), which is the quicker door when the question is about one
person rather than one role.

## The protected role

The IT Manager role is protected: it can never lose its last usable holder.
Revoking the last usable IT Manager grant, and disabling, erasing or merging away the account
that holds it, are all refused with a reason, and the check happens in the same step as the
change it blocks, so two officers acting at once cannot slip past it. Usable means live, on an
account that is neither disabled nor erased and has been signed into; a disabled second IT
Manager, or one still waiting for a first sign-in, does not satisfy the guard. Grant a second IT Manager before doing any of these to the first.

## Authenticators

Any role touching money, personal data or safety records is privileged, and a privileged role
does not work until its holder has enrolled an authenticator app: every console request refuses
with "This role needs an authenticator app before it can be used" and a link to set one up. The
Which roles those are is a setting the IT Manager reads on the settings page. The accounts directory shows a banner counting privileged accounts without an
authenticator, so the handover can chase them. A passkey counts as a second factor for the
session it signed in.

## Why a shift beats a grant

Standing permissions are deliberately sparse. What a person may do tonight comes from facts
that are true tonight and false tomorrow:

- **A confirmed shift on tonight's performance** opens the door, the till and the duty
  manager's screen. The show night runs from 04:00 to 04:00 London, so a shift at 01:00 belongs
  to the previous evening.
- **A current training record** on a trainer-granting module makes somebody a trainer; letting
  the record lapse is the whole of taking the standing away.
- **A current membership** (or one in its grace window) unlocks member prices and member-only
  screens.
- **Department leadership** is a fact recorded on the department, not a role.

Nothing needs remembering to revoke, since none of it was granted. The one named exception is
bounded and audited: a Front of House Manager can open the door and the duty manager's screen
(approving a refund for tonight's performance with it), and a Bar Manager the till, without a
shift tonight, and every such use is recorded in the audit trail as the bypass it is.
