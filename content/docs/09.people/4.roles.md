A role is a standing grant for administrative work: the sit-down jobs done at a desk in the
weeks before a show. It is granted by the IT Manager and runs out at the end of the committee
year unless it is marked permanent. This page is the reference for what each role reaches and
how the register is worked. It is for the reader who has just taken over a role and wants to
know what it opens, and for the IT Manager working out which role somebody needs.

::callout{icon="i-lucide-info" color="info"}
**A role opens the console, not the night.** The door, the till and the duty manager's screen
open to whoever holds tonight's confirmed shift, and a trainer is anybody holding a current
record on a trainer-granting module. No role is a workaround for a missing shift or record: the
fix is the shift or the record.
::

## The roles

There are seven roles. Each one is called the same thing wherever it is read: in the grant list,
in a filter, in a refusal and here. A committee post holds one of them. Five posts have standing
work in the console and a role named after the post. Every other post holds Committee. The IT
Manager is not a post: it is the Archivist's charge over the theatre's IT (clause 4.17.1 of the
constitution), held by the Archivist and the one person they bring in to help.

| Role | Held by | What it opens |
| --- | --- | --- |
| IT Manager | The Archivist (4.17.1, 4.17.2) and their IT delegate (4.17.1) | Everything: every screen and every change below, and the recovery route for any other post. Only the IT Manager creates, disables, merges and erases accounts, grants and revokes roles, changes settings, records and reads backups and restore drills, sends announcements and reads the send log, reopens a closed period, writes the membership register and the roll of Fellows, narrows a pass type that already has live passes, and approves a comp at the desk on a night they are not duty manager. |
| President | The President (4.1) | Reading accounts; reading the audit trail and recording an entry on it by hand; reading the open safety items, including each incident's own account (4.1.1 shares safety with the Theatre Manager); cross-season reports; the money dashboard's totals. It closes nothing, writes no money and grants no role. |
| Secretary and Welfare Officer | The Secretary (4.2) | Verifying access profile declarations: sighting evidence and agreeing the door's wording (4.2.1, the official welfare officer), never on their own declaration; reading the roll of Fellows (3.7.1); cross-season reports; the money dashboard's totals. |
| Treasurer | The Treasurer (4.3) | Reading the ledger and every report built on it; recording a Z reading, resolving a variance and writing one off (4.3.2); closing a period; the SU accounting export; cross-season reports; the money dashboard's totals. It never takes the money. |

Every post role also has what Committee has, so a post holder needs one grant, not two. A list
that names the Committee, such as the roles given bar tab credit, the night report's recipients or
an announcement's audience, reaches every post role too. The IT Manager's role is not the
Committee, so the Archivist holds Committee for the post as well.

A role earns a permission when the thing it unlocks exists. Where a screen is missing from a
role's list, that is deliberate rather than an oversight, and the change is a decision for the
IT Manager, not a grant made in the meantime.

### Retired roles

These roles can no longer be granted. Every grant of one was moved to its successor, and an audit
entry written before then still names it.

- **Manager**: refunds went to the Front of House Manager; the rooms and the training catalogue to
  the Theatre Manager; the audit trail to the President; reading the roll of Fellows to the
  Secretary and Welfare Officer; writing the membership register and the roll, and narrowing a
  pass type with live passes, to the IT Manager alone.
- **Bar Manager**: the whole bar, and opening the till without a shift, went to the Front of House
  Manager.
- **Safety Officer**: safety routing and the open-items list went to the Theatre Manager.
- **Training Manager**: the training catalogue, its leads, revoking and recording by address went
  to the Theatre Manager.
- **Accessibility Officer**: verifying access declarations went to the Secretary and Welfare
  Officer.
- **Box Office Manager** and **Front of House**: the programme and the desk went to the Front of
  House Manager; Front of House granted nothing and was removed.

## The committee year

Every grant expires at the end of the committee year, **31 July, the last instant of the day in
London**, unless it was marked permanent when granted. The expiry is checked every time the
grant is used, not by a nightly job: a grant that lapsed overnight grants nothing from the next
request onwards, and revoking one takes effect the same way. So the handover needs nobody to
remember to revoke anything; what it needs is the incoming committee's grants to be made.

- Holders are warned {{ ROLE_LAPSE_NOTICE_DAYS }} before a grant lapses, once per grant
  and date.
- The IT Manager receives a monthly digest of grants expiring, grants lapsed and the standing
  list of permanent grants, so the exceptions stay visible.
- A grant that lapsed more than {{ ROLE_GRANT_PRUNE_DAYS }} ago is tidied away. The audit
  trail keeps the history; the row is what goes.

A permanent grant is the exception, made by choosing **Further notice** under **Until**, and it
should be rare. The one that is required is an IT Manager's: at least one usable IT Manager always
holds a permanent grant, so the theatre is never without one on 1 August (see the protected role
below). Every permanent grant is listed on the register, across every role, so the
exceptions cannot quietly accumulate. One granted by address before a first sign-in waits under
**Waiting for a first sign-in** instead, and joins the permanent grants once the person signs in.

## The register

**People > Roles** is the register, and the place a handover is worked. It lists every role with
the number of people currently holding it; choosing one shows its holders, until when, who
granted it and any note that was written. The chosen role is part of the address, so a list can
be sent to somebody as a link.

- **Granting.** With a role chosen, search for the person, choose **Until** (the committee year
  end, a date you pick, or further notice) and write a note if the grant needs explaining. The
  committee year end is shown with its date, and in July that date is weeks away: a grant made
  for the incoming committee then wants **A date I pick** and the following 31 July. The
  note sits on the grant for whoever reads the register next; it is never copied into the audit
  trail, which records only that one was written. Every date on the register carries its year.
  For a post other than the IT Manager's, name the post in the note ("Social and Fundraising
  Coordinator"), and for acting cover the clause too. Nobody grants a role to their own account,
  except the IT Manager's: "Nobody grants themselves a role. Ask another IT Manager to make this
  grant."
- **Granting before a first sign-in.** Somebody incoming may have no account yet. When the search
  finds nobody, choose **Nobody found? Grant it by address before their first sign-in**, then give
  their address and name. The address is only offered once the search has come back empty: anybody
  who already has an account is chosen from the search, and typing their address is refused with
  that instruction. So is a theatre address already waiting to be linked to somebody's account:
  the refusal names that account, and that is the one to choose. The grant waits under **Waiting for a first sign-in**, apart from the holders
  and not counted among them, until the person signs in: a theatre address by Google, any other
  address through the link it is sent to set a password (or by registering, which sends the same
  kind of link). It carries the same expiry and note as any grant and can be revoked from that
  list before it is ever used. A waiting IT Manager does not count for the protected role below.
- **Renewing.** Granting a role somebody already has a row for renews that row: the expiry, the
  note and the provenance are replaced and the lapse warning is re-armed. The audit trail records
  it as a renewal, with the old expiry and the new one side by side.
- **Revoking.** Revoke sits beside each holder, and asks first: press **Revoke Treasurer** (the
  role's own name) in the dialogue, or **Back**. It takes effect on the next request, everywhere. A refusal, such as
  the last IT Manager, is said inside that dialogue.
- **Lapsed grants** are hidden until asked for, and the total line says how many were hidden.
  Choose **Show them**, or the **Lapsed** filter, to see them.

The same grants are reachable from a person's own page under
[Accounts](/docs/people/accounts), which is the quicker door when the question is about one
person rather than one role.

## The protected role

The IT Manager role is protected: it can never lose its last usable holder.
Revoking the last usable IT Manager grant, and disabling, erasing or merging away the account
that holds it, are all refused with a reason. The check is made as part of the change itself, so
two officers acting at once cannot both get past it: if the other's change landed first, the
second is refused with "The IT Managers changed while this was being done. Look again, then try
once more." or with the reason that now applies. Usable means live, on an
account that is neither disabled nor erased and has been signed into; a disabled second IT
Manager, or one still waiting for a first sign-in, does not satisfy the guard. Grant a second IT Manager before doing any of these to the first.

A grant that runs out on its date is not something anybody does, so nothing could refuse it: the
last dated IT Manager would simply lapse, with nobody left to grant another. So at least one usable
IT Manager's grant is always permanent, and the register keeps it on the permanent list.

- **Granting the IT Manager role** is refused when, afterwards, no usable IT Manager would hold a
  permanent grant: "No other IT Manager grant is permanent, so this one must be: choose Further
  notice". That includes dating the only permanent grant. Granting it by address, or when
  creating an account, is refused the same way while no IT Manager's grant is permanent: a grant
  waiting for a first sign-in keeps nothing yet. When creating an account the check is made
  before the account is, rather than with the grant: a grant on a new account only ever adds an
  IT Manager, so nothing done at the same moment can make it the one that strands the theatre.
- **Revoking, disabling, erasing or merging away** an IT Manager is refused when no other usable
  IT Manager's grant would be permanent: "No other IT Manager grant is permanent: make one
  permanent before revoking this one". That is the only permanent IT Manager while the others are
  dated, or any IT Manager while every grant is dated. Make one permanent first; a dated IT
  Manager can then go freely.
- **The register warns** with "No IT Manager grant is permanent" at the top of the page whenever
  that is so. The fix is to grant yourself (or another IT Manager) the role again with **Further
  notice**, which renews the grant as a permanent one.

## Authenticators

Any role touching money, personal data or safety records is privileged, and a privileged role
does not work until its holder has enrolled an authenticator app: every console request refuses
with "This role needs an authenticator app before it can be used" and a link to set one up. The
Which roles those are is a setting the IT Manager reads on the settings page. The accounts directory shows a banner counting privileged accounts without an
authenticator, so the handover can chase them. A passkey counts as a second factor for the
session it signed in.

## Why a shift beats a grant

Standing permissions are deliberately sparse. What a person may do tonight comes from facts
that are true tonight and false tomorrow:

- **A confirmed shift on tonight's performance** opens the door, the till and the duty
  manager's screen. The show night runs from 04:00 to 04:00 London, so a shift at 01:00 belongs
  to the previous evening.
- **A current training record** on a trainer-granting module makes somebody a trainer; letting
  the record lapse is the whole of taking the standing away.
- **A current membership** (or one in its grace window) unlocks member prices and member-only
  screens.
- **Department leadership** is a fact recorded on the department, not a role.

Nothing needs remembering to revoke, since none of it was granted. The one named exception is
bounded and audited: the Front of House Manager can open the door, the till and the duty
manager's screen without a shift tonight, and every act on those screens is recorded in the
audit trail as the bypass it is. Closing an earlier night's till is the Front of House Manager's
own standing job, not a bypass. Refunding a paid ticket is not part of it: that is the
Front of House Manager's own standing permission, on any day.
