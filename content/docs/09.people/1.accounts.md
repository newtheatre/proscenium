The accounts directory is every person the system knows: members, ticket guests, officers and
the placeholders the import brought over. It is at **Manage, People, Accounts** and needs the
`accounts.read` permission, which the IT Manager, Manager, Theatre Manager and Training Manager
roles hold. Opening one account shows how they sign in, the roles they hold, their membership
and fellowship, and the last few things done by or to them. The actions on that page are the IT
Manager's: creating an account, granting and revoking roles, disabling, merging and erasing each
need a permission only the IT Manager role holds.

::callout{icon="i-lucide-info" color="info"}
**Two kinds of account are hidden until you look for them.** An anonymised account (one that has
been erased) and a shadow account (one nobody ever registered: no password, no Google link, no
passkey) stay out of the list until a search, their own filter or the never-signed-in filter asks
for them. The total line says how many shadow accounts were left out, with **Show them** beside
it, so hidden never means lost.
::

## Finding an account

![The directory with the search field (1), the filters (2), the Add someone button (3) and the table (4)](/images/docs/people/accounts.png)

1. **Search** by any part of a name, an email address or a student number, then press
   :kbd{value="Enter"}. A search always shows shadow accounts, because somebody typing an
   address wants that address, whoever holds it.
2. **Filters** narrow the list to the questions the handover raises: holds a role (narrowable to
   one role), membership current, lapsed or never, address verified, disabled, anonymised,
   authenticator enrolled, privileged with no authenticator, approaching retention, never
   signed in, shadow account, and last seen within a date range. The sort is by name, last seen
   or joined. Search, filters, sort and page all live in the address bar, so a triage list can be
   bookmarked or sent to somebody.
3. **Add someone** creates an account from the console (below).
4. **The table** shows each account's state badges (Anonymised, Disabled, Shadow, Unverified,
   Authenticator), how it signs in, and when it was last seen. Choose the arrow at the end of a
   row to open the account.
5. **The total line** counts what matched and how many shadow accounts the default view hid.

Two banners appear above the list when there is something to act on: the number of privileged
accounts without an authenticator (their roles do not work until they enrol one) and the number
of accounts dormant for longer than {{ RETENTION_FULL_ACCOUNT_YEARS }} years, which the retention
sweep would warn. Each banner's **Show them** applies the matching filter.

## Adding someone

An account made from the console never gets a password here. The person gets an email with a
link to choose one, and until they do the account is a shadow account, hidden from the default
list: search for it rather than expecting it in the table.

1. Press **Add someone**.
2. Give their **name** and **email address**. A `@newtheatre.org.uk` address signs in with
   Google and gets no link, because no password may ever exist on a Workspace address.
3. Optionally choose **roles** to grant in the same action. Granting needs the `roles.grant`
   permission as well as `accounts.create`, and each grant expires at the committee year end.
4. Press **Create the account**. The link in the email lasts {{ ADMIN_TOKEN_HOURS }} hours.

## One account

![An account with its name and state (1), the Role choice (2), the Until choice (3) and the Grant it button (4)](/images/docs/people/account.png)

1. **The name and state.** Disabled, Shadow or Unverified badges sit beside the name. Beneath
   it, **Signs in with** lists the methods held: a password, Google, passkeys, and an
   authenticator with the number of recovery codes left. "Nothing yet" means the account cannot
   sign in. The **Roles** card lists every grant by its title, with its expiry, or "further
   notice" for a permanent one; a grant that has run out reads as lapsed and grants nothing.
2. **Role** offers the roles the account does not already hold.
3. **Until** is the committee year end, a date you pick, or further notice, and a **Note** may
   be written beside it.
4. **Grant it** writes the grant. All of it shows only to the IT Manager; granting is covered
   below. Beneath the form, **Open the register** opens
   [Roles](/docs/people/roles-and-the-committee-year), which is the better door at handover.

Beneath the roles, **Membership** shows each term recorded against the account, its source and
whether it has been checked against the SU's list, and a Fellow has a **Fellowship** card. Further
down are the **Security** card (only for a reader who holds `accounts.disable`), **Merge into
another account**, and **Recent activity**:
the last 25 audit entries naming this account, each read as a sentence ("Role granted", "Signed
in with a passkey"), marked "(done to them)" where somebody else was the actor. Recent activity is only filled for a reader who also holds `audit.read`.

## Granting and revoking a role

Grants expire at the committee year end (31 July) unless marked permanent. The full list of
roles and what each holds is on [Roles and the committee year](/docs/people/roles-and-the-committee-year).

1. In the **Roles** card, choose a **Role**. A role the account already holds live is not
   offered: renewing one is done from the register.
2. Choose **Until**. The committee year end is the default; **A date I pick** dates the grant to
   the end of that day in London, and **Further notice** makes it permanent. A permanent grant is
   the exception and shows in the IT Manager's monthly digest.
3. Write a **Note** if the grant needs explaining. It stays on the grant and never reaches the
   audit trail, which records only that one was written.
4. Press **Grant it**. The new grant appears with its expiry.
5. To take a role away, press **Revoke** beside it, then **Revoke Box office** in the dialogue.
   The change takes effect on the person's next request.

## Security operations

![The security card with Sign out everywhere (1), Disable the account (2), Reset the authenticator (3), Erase this account (4) and the merge search (5)](/images/docs/people/account-security.png)

Each of these takes effect on the person's next request, everywhere they are signed in, and each
needs the `accounts.disable` permission. The whole card shows only to a reader who holds it: a
Manager, Theatre Manager or Training Manager reading an account sees the roles, the membership
and the history, and no security card at all.

1. **Sign out everywhere** ends every session on the account. Their roles and methods are
   untouched; they sign in again as normal.
2. **Disable the account** ends every session and refuses every sign-in path with the same
   answer as a wrong password, so somebody holding the credentials learns nothing. It asks first:
   press **Disable the account** in the dialogue, or **Back**. **Enable the account** reverses
   it and asks nothing, but the old sessions stay dead until the person signs in again.
3. **Reset the authenticator** clears the authenticator app, every recovery code and every
   session in one batch, for a person who has lost their phone. It asks first too, because an
   officer holding a privileged role cannot sign in again until they enrol a new one.
4. **Erase this account** is anonymisation, described below.
5. **Merge into another account** is described below.

## Erasing an account

Erasure is anonymisation in one transaction, the same path a person takes when they close their
own account. Bookings, training records and shifts stay as anonymous rows so the statistics
survive; the name, address and every piece of personal free text are rewritten or removed; the
access profile and other consented data are deleted outright. Nothing can undo it, and nothing
can write personal data back over the tombstone.

1. Press **Erase this account**. The button only shows for a reader holding `accounts.disable`,
   and never on an account already erased.
2. Read the warning, then type the account's **email address** exactly to confirm. The button
   stays disabled until it matches.
3. Press **Erase the account**.

There is no console export of another person's data. Somebody who wants everything the theatre
holds on them downloads it themselves from their own account's security page, and that download
is audited (see [Your account](/docs/getting-started/your-account)).

## Merging duplicate accounts

The account you are looking at is always the losing side: it moves into the winner and is left
as a tombstone, so anything that referred to it still resolves. Bookings, training records,
shifts, memberships and role grants move; the loser's sign-in methods and authenticator are
retired, never combined with the winner's. The whole merge is one transaction, so a failure
changes nothing.

1. In **Merge into another account**, type the **winning account's email** exactly and press
   **Preview the merge**. Nothing changes yet.
2. The preview lists exactly what will move: the number of bookings, training records, shifts,
   memberships and role grants.
3. Type the **losing account's email** (the one on this page) to confirm, and press **Merge the
   accounts**.

## Shadow accounts

A shadow account has no way to sign in: no password, no Google link, no passkey. Three things
make one: adding somebody from the console before they choose a password, a ticket guest who
gave a name and an address at the box office, and the import, which brought over everyone the
old system knew, most of whom only ever bought a ticket. They are badged **Shadow**, never
Unverified, because nobody registered them to verify. The state ends by itself the moment a
password, a Google link or a passkey is written; nothing needs tidying.

## If something goes wrong

- **"You do not have permission to do that"**: the action behind the button is the IT Manager's.
  Ask them. The security operations and erasure are not shown at all to a reader without
  `accounts.disable`, so this answer now comes only from **Merge into another account**, which
  needs `accounts.merge` and is still offered to anyone who can open the page.
- **"This role needs an authenticator app before it can be used"**: your own role is privileged
  and you have not enrolled an authenticator. The alert links to the security page to set one up.
- **"That address already has an account"**: search for it instead; it may be a shadow account
  hidden from the default list.
- **"Nothing can be delivered to that address"**: the address is a placeholder or an
  undeliverable domain, so a set-password link could never arrive.
- **"You do not have permission to grant roles"**: creating the account is allowed but the roles
  chosen with it are not. Create it without roles.
- **"That account is no longer here."** when granting a role: the account has been erased. A tombstone can hold
  no role.
- **"That is the last administrator: grant another before revoking this one"** (or disabling,
  erasing, or merging this one away): the system will never lose its last usable IT Manager. A
  disabled or erased second administrator does not count. Grant another first.
- **"That is your own account"**: security operations, erasure and merges refuse to target the
  person doing them, so nobody locks themselves out by accident.
- **"That account has been erased"**: nothing acts on a tombstone.
- **"No account matches that email exactly."**: the winner's address must match an account in
  full; a partial match is not offered, because the wrong winner cannot be undone.
- **"An account cannot be merged into itself"** and **"An anonymised account cannot take part in
  a merge"**: pick a different winner.
- **"Type the losing account's email address exactly to confirm"**: the confirmation is checked
  again on the server, character for character after trimming and lower-casing.
- **"That account changed since it was last checked: look at it again before merging"**: the
  losing account was altered between the preview and the merge. Reload and preview again.

## What happens next

Every action here is in the audit trail with who did it and to whom: `account.created.console`,
`role.granted` (with its expiry, and whether it was permanent), `role.renewed` (with the old
expiry and the new one), `role.revoked`,
`account.disabled`, `account.enabled`, `session.revoked`, `mfa.reset`, `account.erased.admin`
and `account.merged` with the counts that moved. A new account is emailed its set-password link
unless it is a Workspace address. A revoked or lapsed grant grants nothing from the next request
onwards; nothing waits for a nightly job.
