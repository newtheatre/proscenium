Comps and discounts is at **Manage, Money, Comps and discounts**, and the Treasurer reads it. A giveaway is a figure here, never a silent gap: a comp posts to the ledger
at 0 with the full price kept on its line, and a discount keeps the amount it took off, so
what was forgone can be added up later without reconstructing any price history.

## If something goes wrong

- **"The report could not be read."**: the figures did not come back, or your role no longer
  reads the ledger. Change the scope and back to read it again.
- **"Choose a show to see its figures."**: the scope is **One show** and none is chosen yet.
- **The value looks too high for a comp**: the value column is what the seat or the drink would
  have sold for, not what was taken (which was nothing). That is what forgone means.

## Choosing a scope

![Comps and discounts with the scope (1), the date range (2), forgone value (3) and access admissions (4)](/images/docs/money/comps-and-discounts.png)

1. Choose the **scope**: **A date range**, or **One show** for one production across all
   its performances.
2. For a period, set **from** and **to** (London dates; the screen opens on today). For a show,
   pick it from the list; until you do, the screen says "Choose a show to see its figures."
   Changing either reads the ledger again at once; there is nothing else to press.
3. **Forgone value** has two rows. **Comps**: how many comp lines were given and what they would
   have sold for. **Discounts**: how many lines carried a discount and how much was taken off.
4. **Access and companion admissions** lists each access kind of ticket with a count and the
   value taken. It reads "None in this scope." when there were none.

::callout{icon="i-lucide-shield" color="info"}
Access admissions are counts and value only. Nothing on this screen, or in the report behind it,
reaches a person's declared needs or their name; only the Accessibility Officer sees those.
::

## Where a comp comes from

The Treasurer reads comps; nobody gives one from here. At the desk a comp needs a request with
a reason, approved by a manager before the COMP tender goes through. At the bar a comp needs a
request approved by tonight's duty manager or the Bar Manager, never the person who asked.
Refunding a paid ticket at the desk needs the Manager, or tonight's confirmed duty manager,
where the settings say so. The approver's identity travels on the ledger entry.

## What happens next

Nothing is written by reading this screen. The same comp and discount totals appear on the
money dashboard for the same range and in the [daily reconciliation](/docs/money/daily-reconciliation)
split for each night, where they are already netted out of the figure the reader is expected to
show.
