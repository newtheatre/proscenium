Daily reconciliation is at **Manage, Money, Daily reconciliation**, and the Treasurer reads it
and records a reading on it. Nothing comes back from the card reader on its own, so this is a
person's job: once a night, read the Z total off the SU's SumUp reader and type it in. What it
should say is already worked out for you.

::callout{icon="i-lucide-info" color="info"}
A **night**, not a calendar day. The show night runs from 04:00 to 04:00 London time, so a sale
rung through at 00:30 belongs to the evening before, the same night the reader was read for. The
month and year totals elsewhere group by the plain London calendar day instead; that is why a
night's figure and a day's figure can legitimately differ.
::

## If something goes wrong

- **"The reader read £…; we expect £…. That difference needs a note before it can be
  recorded."**: the two disagree and the note is empty. Say why, then record. The screen holds
  **Record** back until there is a note, so this only appears if a sale landed while you typed.
- **"A write-off must name the variance it resolves, and cannot write off a figure that now
  matches"**: the reading already matches what we expect. Press **Check again** instead.
- **"A write-off names the variance it resolves: record or reload the night's reading first"**:
  the reading being written off is no longer on the screen. Reload the night.
- **"The screen expected £…; the ledger now reads £…. Nothing was written off: check the night
  and try again."**: a sale or a refund reached the night after the screen was opened, so the
  variance is not the one the button named. Reload the night and read the new figure before
  writing it off.
- **"That reading is already written off"**: somebody wrote it off first. Reload the night.
- **"That is not a real date"**: the night in the address does not exist, such as 30 February.
  The screen opens on tonight instead.
- **"Somebody else just recorded this night's first reading; resolve it instead of adding
  another"**: two people reconciled the same night at once. Reload the screen and, if the
  other reading is wrong, correct it.
- **"That reading already has a correction or a write-off resolving it"** or **"That reading is
  no longer the live one for this night"**: the screen was stale. Reload the screen and resolve
  the reading that is now current.
- **"That reading could not be recorded."**: any other failure. Reload and try once more;
  if it persists, report the page.

## Reading what the ledger expects

![Daily reconciliation with the night (1), the expected figure (2), the current reading (3), the reader total (4) and Record (5)](/images/docs/money/reconciliation.png)

1. Pick the **night**. The screen opens on the current show night, or on the night its address
   names: every night listed anywhere links straight to its own reconciliation. If any night up
   to tonight took card money with no reading, or still carries an open variance, **Needs you:
   nights to reconcile** lists them all at the top, oldest first, each linked and saying which:
   **No reading** or **Open variance**. The list is never cut short. A night counts as taking money
   when the desk or the bar put any card payment through the reader, whether or not a show was
   on or a till was opened: a pass sold on a quiet afternoon, or a tab settled at the bar with
   no till session, puts that night on the list. A night with only comps, tab charges or
   imported history on it does not. Nights with no reading are listed from
   **1 September 2026**, the first night that had to be reconciled here: nights imported from
   the old system never had a Z reading. A reading somebody did record for an earlier night
   still appears if its variance is open.
2. **Expected for the night** is built from the ledger alone: the desk's card takings by kind
   (ticket collections, walk-up sales, pass sales), the desk total, the bar's card sales and its
   tab settlements, and the **Expected total**. Comps, discounts and bar refunds are printed
   beneath the table for information, and are already netted out of the total: a comp never
   moved money through the reader.
3. **Current reading** says whether a reading exists for the night, and if so what the reader
   showed, the variance (reader less expected), who entered it and whether it was written off.
   If what we expect has changed since, a line beneath says what the variance is now.

## A card charge left on a closed till

Sometimes a bar charges a card and nobody answers whether it went through before the till is
closed. The bar can no longer record that sale, so it is yours. **Card charges left on a closed
till** lists each one for the night: the amount, the bar, the time the reader took it, who charged
it, and where it stands.

1. Check the reader's Z for the night. If it holds the money, press **Payment went through: record
   it on this night**.
2. The sale lands on this night, as the bar sale it was: dated when the reader took it, keyed to
   the night's performance, on the till it was rung up against. **Expected for the night** rises
   by the amount, so a variance the missing sale caused closes with **Check again**.
3. The trail records it as a late record, naming you and when you recorded it. It is listed under
   **Late additions** here, and the night report shows it too: as a late addition, or as an
   addendum if the report was already signed off.

If the charge did not go through, leave it: the Bar Manager answers **Payment did not** from the
till. Only the Treasurer, or an administrator, can record one; the Bar Manager's till row leads
here.

- **"The screen said £…; this charge is £…. Nothing was recorded: read the night again."**: the
  screen was stale. Reload the night and check the figure before recording.
- **"That charge has been answered, or its night's till reopened, since the screen was read."**:
  somebody answered it first, or the bar reopened that night's till and can record it there.
  Reload the night.
- A charge that can no longer be sold as it stood (the booking was collected at the desk
  meanwhile, say) is marked mismatched with the reason, and nothing is recorded; the Bar Manager
  abandons it with a note saying what happened to the money.

## Recording the night's reading

On a night the bar ran, the reading is already there: closing the till asks for the reader's Z
against the whole night, and records that figure and the closer's note as the night's reading,
entered by the closer. If a second bar closes later the same night, its fuller reading replaces
the first. A reading you record, correct or write off always stands; a till closed after it
records nothing here. So you type a Z only for a night no till closed, or to resolve one:

1. Read the Z total off the reader.
2. Type it into **Reader total (Z)**, in pounds and pence, as the reader shows it: `123.45`. A
   pound sign and a thousands comma are both read through, and the line beneath the field
   repeats the figure being recorded so you can check it before pressing Record. If the reader
   shows nothing at all, press **Reader shows £0.00** instead of typing.
3. As you type, a line says whether the figure matches what we expect, or by how much it
   differs.
4. If it matches, press **Record**. Nothing else is needed.
5. If it does not match, write a **Note** saying why. **Record** waits until there is one.

::callout{icon="i-lucide-triangle-alert" color="warning"}
A bare number is pounds, so `123` records £123.00. Anything the field cannot read as pounds and
pence is refused before Record can be pressed. The expected figure is worked out again at
the moment you press Record, never taken from what the screen showed, so a sale rung through
while you were reading the reader is already counted.
::

## Resolving a variance

A reading with a variance stays open until it is resolved. While one is open, **Resolve the
variance** offers three separate actions, and none of them asks you to type the reader total
again unless it was wrong:

1. **Check again**: once the missing sale or refund has reached the ledger, what we expect may
   now equal what the reader showed. When it does, the screen says so and **Check again**
   records the same figure against the new expected total, closing the variance.
2. **Write off**: the difference is real and accepted. Write why, and press **Write off £…**.
   The reading is accepted as it stands: the figure comes from the reading itself. If what we
   expect has moved since the screen opened, nothing is written off and the screen says both
   figures. A reading already written off cannot be written off again; it can still be corrected.
3. **Correct the reading**: the first figure was mistyped. Type the right reader total and a
   note if it still differs, and press **Record the correction**. This is offered for any
   reading, open or not.

Each names the reading it resolves; both stay in the history.

The **History** table shows every reading for the night in order, oldest first, with the reader
figure, the variance (and whether it was written off), who entered it and the note. Readings
are never edited or deleted.

## What happens next

Each reading is a row in the reconciliation record with the night, the reader total, the
expected figure, the variance, who entered it, when, the note and what it supersedes. The
same step puts **Daily Z reading recorded** on the audit trail. A night with an open
variance stays on the **Needs you** list here, on the money dashboard and on the console
overview, adds to the dashboard's **Open
variance** figure, and is named as a warning when somebody tries to
[close a period](/docs/money/periods) covering it.
