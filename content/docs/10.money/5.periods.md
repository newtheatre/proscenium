Periods is at **Manage, Money, Periods**, and the Treasurer reads the close history and closes
a period. Reopening one is the IT Manager's alone: no other role does it at all, and the screen
says so to anyone who cannot.

A period is any range of London days the Treasurer names, with an optional label such as a term
name. Closing it does not change a single entry inside it. It adds a row to the close history,
and from that moment the ledger refuses any new entry dated inside the range, wherever in the
system somebody tries to post one. A report over a closed period therefore gives the same figures
every time it is run.

## If something goes wrong

- **"A period cannot end before it starts"**: the To date is earlier than the From date.
- **"Give the period a label"**: a label was started but is only spaces. Fill it in or clear it.
- **"A term cannot end before it starts"** or **"Give the term a label"**: the same two checks on
  the term form.
- **"Keep the label to 120 characters"**: shorten the label.
- **"Some of these days are already closed: …. Close only the days outside it, or reopen it
  first."**: part of the range is already closed by the close it names. Every day has one close
  in force, so close only the days either side of it, or have the IT Manager reopen it first. The
  preview says this before you press Close.
- **"A term with that label or those dates is already defined. Use that one, or remove it
  first."**: the term exists already. Pick it from the list, or remove it and name it again.
- **"The close history changed while you were closing. Reload the page and try again."**: somebody
  closed or reopened part of the range at the same moment. Reload and look again.
- **"The typed range does not match this lock: check what you are reopening and try again"**:
  the dates you typed are not the row's own. Read them off the table again.
- **"This period is not currently closed"**: somebody reopened it, or a newer row now governs
  every day of it, since the screen loaded. Reload the page.
- **"The close history could not be read."**: the list did not come back, or your role no
  longer reads the ledger. Reload the page.
- **"This period is closed: post a correction in the open period instead."**: shown on a
  money-taking screen, not here, when somebody posts money dated inside a closed range. Post it
  in the open period, or have the IT Manager reopen the range if the date itself is right.

## Reading the close history

![Periods with Close a period (1) and the close history (2)](/images/docs/money/periods.png)

1. **Define a term** and **Close a period** open their forms, and show only to the Treasurer and
   the IT Manager.
   **Defined terms**, above the table, lists every term already named.
2. The table lists every close and every reopen, newest first: the range, its label, its
   **Standing**, and who did it and when. The newest row covering a day is what governs that day,
   however many times it has been closed and reopened, and the standing says what each row
   still does:
   - **Closed**: the row governs every day of its range.
   - **Partly closed**: a later row governs some of its days, and it still closes the rest.
   - **Reopened**: the row opened its range and still governs it.
   - **Closed, no longer in force** or **Reopened, no longer in force**: later rows govern every
     day of it. It stays in the history, but changes nothing.

   This is the same rule the ledger and the [SU export](/docs/money/exports) use, so the three
   always agree about which days are closed. A **Reopen** button appears on a row that is Closed
   or Partly closed, and only for the IT Manager.

## Naming a term

A term is a named range and nothing more: it is what the [money dashboard](/docs/money)'s
**TERM** period reports on, and the dashboard offers TERM only once at least one term exists.
Naming a term neither closes the range nor is needed before closing it.

1. Press **Define a term**.
2. Give the **Label**, for example "Autumn 2026".
3. Set **From** and **To**. The range cannot end before it starts.
4. Press **Define the term**.

A term is named once: a second term with the same label or the same dates is refused. If a term
was named wrongly, press **Remove** beside it and confirm, then name it again. A term is only a
name for a range, so removing one changes no figure and opens or closes nothing.

## Closing a period

![The close form with From (1), To (2), Label (3) and the preview (4)](/images/docs/money/periods-close.png)

1. Press **Close a period**.
2. Set **From** and **To**. The range cannot end before it starts.
3. Add a **Label** if you want one, for example a term name.
4. Press **Preview what this warns about**. The screen reads the reconciliation record and
   says either "No unreconciled nights or open variances in this range." or **This range has
   open items**, counting the nights that took card money with no recorded Z reading (the same
   list [daily reconciliation](/docs/money/daily-reconciliation) shows) and the nights with an open
   variance, and names each night with a link to its reconciliation. A night before
   1 September 2026 with no Z reading is imported history with nothing to record, so it never
   counts; a reading recorded for such a night with an open variance still does. If any day of
   the range is already closed, the preview says which close covers it instead, and **Close this
   period** stays unavailable until the range leaves those days out.
5. Press **Close this period**, or **Back** to change the range.

::callout{icon="i-lucide-triangle-alert" color="warning"}
The preview is a warning, not a refusal. Closing past open items does not resolve them: it only
stops new entries landing inside the range. Reconcile the nights first where you can, from
[daily reconciliation](/docs/money/daily-reconciliation); if you close anyway, a later correction
posts in the open period.
::

## Reopening a period

Only the IT Manager sees **Reopen**. It is a typed confirmation: the range is read back from
the row you are reopening and compared with what you type, so a stale screen cannot reopen a
different period from the one in front of you.

1. Press **Reopen** on the closed range.
2. Enter the range's **start** and **end** dates in the two date fields; each field's label
   names the date it wants. **Reopen the period** stays disabled until both match.
3. Press **Reopen the period**. Nothing else is undone: the close row stays in the history, and
   a new **Reopened** row with the same label is added above it. Closing again later is another
   new row.

## What happens next

The audit trail reads **Term defined** when a term is named, **Term removed** when one is
removed, **Period closed** on a close and **Period reopened** on a reopen, each with the range and who did it. The SU export marks whether the
range it covers is closed, so the Students' Union can tell a stable return from a provisional
one; see [exports](/docs/money/exports).
