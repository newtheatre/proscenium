Two screens under **My NNT** cover a signed-in member's training. **My training** is what they
hold, how long each record is good for, what they could do next and what they have asked for.
**Training sessions** is the schedule: what is being taught, and where they stand on each one.
The public catalogue at `/training/modules` describes every module, what it needs first and
what it leads to, and signed in it also says which ones you hold.

## Reading your records

![My training on a phone, with the records grouped by department (1) and the sessions signed up to (2)](/images/docs/members/training.png)

1. **Records**, grouped by department. Each shows its module id and name, its kind (Module,
   Certification or Brief), how it was come by (Session award, External certificate,
   Self-registered, Brought over or Signed off), when it was awarded and when it runs to.
2. **What you are signed up to**: each session with "You have a place" or "Waiting, number N".

Below them, **What you could do next** lists every module whose prerequisites you already hold
and which you do not yet hold yourself, and **What you have asked for** lists your requests.

A record is in one of three states, worked out from its dates each time the page loads, never
stored:

- **Valid**.
- **Renew soon**: within {{ TRAINING_EXPIRY_WARNING_DAYS }} days of its expiry. It still counts
  for everything until its date, so there is nothing to do today; a warning is emailed as the
  date approaches.
- **Expired**: on and after its expiry date. "It has not been taken away: it has stopped counting
  towards the things that need it."

A brief never expires, so it shows its last attended date instead. Anything expired or expiring
is called out at the top of the page before the list, each with an **Ask for** button naming the
module that starts a request for it. Every entry under **What you could do next** carries the
same button, so a suggestion is something you can act on where you read it. A module you have
already asked for reads **Asked for** and cannot be asked for twice.

"You can run training sessions" (or "supervise") appears when a certification you hold carries
that standing. It follows the record and lasts exactly as long as it does.

## Signing up for a session

![Training sessions on a phone, with the sessions coming up (1), a session's card (2) and its sign-up button (3)](/images/docs/members/training-sessions.png)

1. **Coming up** lists every session open to sign up for.
2. **A session**: the modules it teaches, the day and times, the place, the trainer, and how
   many places are left.
3. **Sign up**, or **Join the waiting list** when the session is full.

To sign up:

1. Open **Training sessions** under **My NNT**.
2. Find the session and press **Sign up**. "You have a place" or "Waiting, number N" says
   exactly where you stand at that moment, and what follows says the same: a place tells you how
   to give it up, and a waiting place says one opens for you if somebody withdraws. There is no separate waiting list: a place is your
   position in sign-up order against the session's capacity, so a full session takes you rather
   than turning you away, and a withdrawal ahead of you moves you up.
3. The session moves to **What you are signed up to**, on both screens. **Withdraw** gives the
   place to whoever is next; signing up again puts you at the back.

Sign-up closes {{ SESSION_SIGNUP_CLOSES_HOURS }} hours before the session starts, when the day
arrives, or when the trainer opens the register, whichever is first. A closed session says why.
Withdrawing stays open while the register is.

A session for a safety-critical module needs its prerequisites first: "This one is safety
critical, so it needs X first" and there is no button. For an ordinary module a missing
prerequisite is a note, not a block: "This usually follows X. You can still come: the trainer
will know whether it matters on the night."

## Asking for a module

Asking tells the department there is demand. It holds no place and no priority, and never
expires on its own.

1. On **My training**, press **Ask for** beside an expired or expiring record, or ask from a
   module's page in the catalogue.
2. Say anything worth saying (when you are free, why you need it, who else wants it). It is
   optional.
3. Press **Ask**. "Asked. A lead will see it on their board." The request appears under
   **What you have asked for** as Waiting; **Withdraw** takes it back.

When a session teaching that module opens for sign-up, the request reads Scheduled and you are
emailed once. A lead may also answer it; the answer and its reason appear under the request.

## If something goes wrong

- **"You hold no training records yet."**: nothing has been awarded to this account. Records
  appear as they are earned.
- **"You have already asked for this one, and it is still waiting"**: one open request per
  module. Withdraw it to ask again.
- **"That module is not being taught at the moment, so there is nothing to ask for"**: the
  module is draft or retired.
- **"Sign-up for this one has not opened yet."**, **"Sign-up has closed so the trainer knows
  their numbers."**, **"Sign-up closed when the day arrived."**, **"The register is open, so
  sign-up has closed."**: the session is not taking sign-ups. Speak to the trainer on the night.
- **"This one is safety critical, so it needs X first."**: ask for those modules and come to
  the next one.
- **"That sign-up did not stick. Please try again."**: the write was lost to a concurrent
  change. Press the button again.
- **"Your training records could not be read."**, **"The schedule could not be read."**: the
  list did not arrive, which is not the same as there being nothing on it. Press **Try again**.

## What happens next

A record is awarded by the trainer marking the register, never by signing up. Every gate in the
system (a shift claim, the till) reads the same records with the same rules, so an ability
never flickers off before its date. A sign-up records its position; a withdrawal promotes the
next person and emails them once. A request lands on the department's demand board, ordered by
how many have asked. Expiry warnings and monthly digests come from the training sweep.
